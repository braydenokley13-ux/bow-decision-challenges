New York State Education Department 
 Personal Finance Education 
Instructional Resources 

Section 100.2(c)(13) of the Commissioner’s Regulations requires public schools to provide instruction in 
personal finance education to students in grades Kindergarten through 12. Such instruction must be 
provided to elementary school students by the end of grade 4, to middle school students by the end of 
grade 8, and to high school students by the end of grade 12. For more information and resources, visit 
the New York State Education Department (NYSED) Personal Finance Education Webpage.  

This instructional resource list includes a variety of personal finance tools and materials that districts, 
schools, and teachers may choose to use to support instruction. The resources are provided for 
informational purposes only and are not mandated by NYSED. The resources are organized into two 
categories: 

■  Curricula: Evidence-based instructional resources that include the scope and sequence of 

learning experiences taught in a course or grade level to provide students with opportunities to 
achieve knowledge and skills as defined by applicable learning standards. Typically divided into 
units and lessons, curricula include an outline of the scope of content, concepts, and skills to be 
taught, the sequence in which they are to be taught, the amount of time allocated for the 
content, and the materials and assessments to be used throughout instruction. In New York 
State, curricula are adopted locally and must be aligned with applicable State Learning 
Standards.  

■  Curricular materials: Instructional resources used to support teaching and learning in a 

classroom. These can include, but are not limited to, textbooks, multimedia content, online 
resources, workbooks, teacher manuals, and lesson plans. 

Because districts may implement personal finance instruction in different ways, each resource was 
reviewed and aligned to one or more suggested implementation options: 

■  Embedded Instruction: Instruction is integrated into required subject areas/courses (e.g., 

mathematics, science, social studies, and English Language Arts). 

■  Stand-alone Course: Instruction on personal finance is provided in a dedicated course(s). 

■  Career and Technical Education (CTE) Programming: Instruction connected to Career and 

Technical Education (CTE) programming and career-focused learning. 

In addition, each resource was reviewed for alignment to the five required personal finance topic areas: 
1) Budgeting and Money Management, 2) Credit and Debt Management, 3) Earning Income, 4) Risk 
Management, and 5) Saving and Investing. 

Finally, resources were categorized by the grade bands for which materials are relevant. Resources were 
confirmed to be free for use, though several may have enhanced features that come at a cost.

The New York State Education Department does not mandate specific curricula.

Curricular Materials  

Curricular materials may include, but are not limited to: textbooks, multimedia materials, online resources, workbooks, teacher manuals, and lesson 
plans. Curricular materials can be delivered through a stand‑alone course, embedded within required coursework, or delivered within Career and 
Technical Education programming. 

The table below includes “clearinghouse” websites that provide access to free resources from multiple providers for a variety of grade bands and 
implementation options. They enable users to filter resources and find curricular materials that align to the five required personal finance topics and to 
grade-band learning objectives.
