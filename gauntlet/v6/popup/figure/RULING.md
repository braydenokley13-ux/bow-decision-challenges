# Ruling — the figure at the pass

Judged against `gauntlet/v5/art/pass/lane-master.html` (NEAR PASSERS-BY, ~line 758, and the
`<defs>` figure block, ~line 249), the two Director's-note sections of `gauntlet/v6/POPUP_BRIEF.md`,
and `gauntlet/v6/popup/DIRECTOR_FINDINGS.md`. Every PNG was opened and inspected, including at
2–6x crops of the head, the arm and the crop line.

**Verdict: F1 wins, conditionally. F2 and F3 do not clear the bar and should not be salvaged.**

---

## The standard

> A district visitor should not be able to tell that the person at the window was drawn by a
> different hand from the people in the lane behind them.

Two of the three sheets answer that question themselves, by putting their figure next to the
plate's own baked people in the same frame. Those two exhibits decided most of this ruling.

---

## 1 · Same hand as the plate's near passers-by?

**F1 — yes.** `F1/sheet.png`, last cell, stands the figure beside the plate's own people. It sits
in the same value range (a dark near-silhouette, no local colour, nothing approaching pure black),
its rim strokes are in the plate's own width family (2.8–4.6 through a 2.4 blur, against the
plate's 1.8–2 through `b1`, at ~1.6x the plate figure's scale), and its silhouette is built from
the plate's own primitives rather than from a smooth mass. Two of its rim strokes (5 and 6.2) run
hotter than the plate ever does — see the conditions below — but the family is right.

**F2 — no.** The figure is barely lit at all. `F2/detail.png` and the `#1 served` cell show a
silhouette dimmer than the plate's *background stall vendors*, which inverts the brief's whole
dominance argument ("a customer facing the window is facing our lamp, so their front is the
brightest thing in the frame … get it backwards and the figure reads as a cut-out"). It reads as a
cut-out. Separately, the torso is a downward wedge that narrows to a flat point at the crop line —
a chess pawn, not a coat. The plate's own coat path is near-straight-sided and *widens* toward the
hem (`M457,350 … 453,396 … 450,438`: 42 units across the shoulders, 52 at the hem). F2 authored the
opposite shape. Its own weakness note concedes "conical."

**F3 — no, and its own exhibit says so.** The bottom strip of `F3/sheet.png` puts the plate's
verbatim near passer-by on the left and F3's customer on the right. Left: a quiet dark shape with
one faint hairline rim. Right: a glowing wireframe — thick blurred tubes looping over the crown,
around the shoulder, and down both arms as bright triangles. Worse, the strokes **miss the
silhouette edge**, which is precisely what the approach claimed to have made impossible: at 3x
(`F3/detail.png`, head crop) the head rim sweeps *across the interior* of the skull like a hook,
and the near arm's rim runs down the middle of the arm rather than its edge. This is the single
clearest failure of the headline test on the table.

F1 is the only one of the three where all four items from the findings table are present *and*
legible.

## 3 · Three feet away, or leaning through the hatch?

**F1: three feet away.** Crown-to-crop lands at roughly 2x the plate's near passers-by's visible
height, cropped at the waist on the counter's near lip, with clear sky above the head and the teal
stall still readable behind it. It no longer crowds the stalls, which was the findings' complaint.

**F2** is acceptably scaled but the wedge makes the reading moot. **F3** is too large — the head
alone eats a significant fraction of the hatch, and the glow enlarges it further.

## 4 · Four outcomes from posture alone, captions covered

**F1: yes.** Laid out side by side with the labels cropped off (`waiting / served / short /
no-stock`), *served* swings the elbow wide with the forearm reaching in and opens a triangle of lit
lane; *short* drops the shoulders and hangs the arm off the counter at the side; *no-stock* rotates
the torso off the window with the head shifted, dropped and tilted; *no-hands* is a different
picture entirely — walking figures out on the lane with legs, shin-cropped, at the edge of the lamp
pool. Stock loss and capacity loss are unmistakably different events. *short* versus *waiting* is
the subtle pair, but *waiting* is not one of the four outcomes.

**F2**: served/short/no-stock differ mainly in where a stiff arm points; **F3**: served and waiting
read alike under the glow.

## 5 · Does a group of three read as a group?

**F1: yes.** Lead forward and taller, companions offset, overlapping, cut higher up their own
bodies by the same fixed counter lip, and dimmer. Not a row of equals. The two companions sit a
little symmetrically either side of the lead — worth nudging, not a defect.

F2's and F3's groups read as a group too, but both inherit their lead figure's problems.

## 6 · Anything that could be read as a face?

**This is the one thing that must change in F1 before it ships.** At 6x (`F1/detail.png`, head
crop) the cap brim is a dark band across the head at roughly brow height, and the `#0c0805` jaw
shadow is a dark oval sitting at the base of the head-mass where the head and the neck column fuse
in value. Read together: a band across the eyes and a mouth. Structurally they are a brim and a
collar shadow; perceptually, at 300–400% zoom, they can resolve as a face. Rule 4 of the brief is a
child-safety decision, not a style note, so this is a required fix, not a nit. It is two numbers.

F3 is worse on this axis — brim at the brow, a dark blob under the chin, and a bright arc slashing
across the skull between them. F2 has the same brim-at-eye-line band, more weakly.

## 7 · Does it hold at all three grades?

**Not proven for F1.** Its sheet has no grade row. F3's sheet does, and that is the one thing worth
taking from F3 — the *format of the proof*, not a line of its drawing. F1 claims verification
across cover / center 64% / bullnose 90.2%; it has to be shown before this lands.

## What is taken from where

- **The drawing: F1, entirely.** Nothing of F2's or F3's geometry survives.
- **The proof sheet: F3's format.** Its three-grade row and its side-by-side-against-the-plate's-
  verbatim-figure exhibit are the right way to evidence this work. F1's sheet should adopt both.
- **F2 contributes nothing.**

## Conditions on F1 before it lands

1. **Kill the face read.** Raise the cap brim so it sits at or above the upper third of the skull
   (or draw it as a hood roll), and either delete the jaw shadow or move it entirely inside the
   neck column so no part of it falls within the head ellipse's footprint. Re-inspect at 400% for
   all five `HEADWEAR` values across all five poses.
2. **Widen the shoulders ~12–15%.** F1 runs about 2.0–2.2x head-width to shoulder-width; the plate
   is 1.6x (head `rx 13` → 26 across, against a 42-across shoulder line). Widen the half-span `S`;
   do not shrink the head — the findings called the old head too small. Crown-to-hip 238 stays.
3. **Cool the two hottest rims.** Bring the 5 and 6.2 strokes down to ≤4.6, and break the warm run
   so it reads as one lamp-side pass rather than a closed outline; the market-side edge carries only
   the single cool `#7d9cc4` rim. The plate never exceeds width 2 at 0.6 opacity.
4. **Redraw the `no-hands` walkers.** They are the weakest cell on the sheet — the leftmost torso
   bulges and the legs are stubs, and beside the plate's own baked passers-by in the same frame
   they lose. Re-derive the stride from `fig-walk` (two strokes, `stroke-width 14`,
   `stroke-linecap round`, one trailing and one reaching) at F1's own 1.56 factor, and move the
   group off the frame edge so a whole figure is visible.
5. **Add the three-grade row** at the shipped hatch geometry (cover, `center 64%`, bullnose 90.2%),
   in F3's format, plus F3's side-by-side-with-the-plate exhibit.
6. **Land the P1 with it.** Out of the drawing's scope but not out of this change's scope:
   `src/stages/popup/RunSaturday.tsx:232` renders `<PassCustomer order={focus} …>` unconditionally,
   and `focus = run.orders[Math.max(0, dealt - 1)]` (line 164) never clears — so the last customer
   is still standing at the pass under "Nobody is waiting." in `after-service-close.png`. Note that
   passing `order={undefined}` is **not** enough: `poseFor` returns `"waiting"` for an undefined
   order and would still draw a figure. Gate the element itself —
   `{!done && <PassCustomer order={focus} resolved={resolved} />}` (`done`, line 143). The rule is:
   the pass is empty when the lane is empty, and it is empty at close.

## Drop-in check

All three keep the export surface byte-compatible (`Person`, `personFor`, `Pose`, `PassCustomer`)
and keep `BUILDS` / `HEADWEAR` / `HEIGHTS` / `LEANS` / `COATS` identical, so seeded identity
survives a replay. F1's and F2's `personFor` are byte-identical to the shipped file; F3's differs
only in a reflowed comment. F1 and F2 import from `../../domain/...` and drop straight in over
`src/stages/popup/PassCustomer.tsx`; F3's import path is rewritten for its sandbox and would need
restoring.
