# Read-and-judge war — Judge 3: mechanical originality and content safety

**Filed 19 Aug 2026.** Read in this order: `READ_AND_JUDGE_WAR_BAR.md`, then the five submissions blind of designer reasoning, then `PORTFOLIO_COURT.md` §1–2 (the six hand-acts, the three families). I did not write any of these designs and I am authorised to reject all five. Two jobs: is the fourth family real, and is the content safe to put in front of an 11-to-13-year-old.

## Job one — is the fourth family real?

### 1–2. Hand-acts and reductions

Stated as verbs a hand does, and tested against the three families that already carry six Worlds:

- **A**: *unfold a labeled edge; search-pull an unlinked node.* Not distribution (no pot, no containers, no constraint). Not a time axis — the design is right that "the countdown is a pressure prop the student can interrogate, not a strip the student places things on." Not bind-a-line-to-a-question: in Ferro's the document is handed over and the act is filing; here the act is *choosing a direction of expansion* and the relationships themselves (paid-by, certified-by, checked-in-registry-by) are the content. This is the "network / investigation board" family `PORTFOLIO_COURT.md` §2 explicitly names as never appearing. **It is real, and A is it.**
- **B**: *drag a swatch into a slot* (Act One — literally `AllocationControl` energy: a tray, slots, a meter) and *drag a tile into a slot bound to a question* (Act Two — "Evidence for / Evidence against / Who benefits" is bind-a-line-to-a-question with the question printed on the slot). B confesses the two halves "share one hand-act." They do, and it's two of the three old ones. **Fails DQ3.**
- **C**: *tap a pill bound to a panel, answer, seal.* "Who benefits if you believe this?" printed under each source panel is family (c) verbatim — the question is fixed and the act is attaching an answer to a document. The genuinely fresh part — sealed prediction, delayed truth, your own words flipped face-up next to the fact — is a *reveal structure*, and the portfolio already owns "advance time and read a printed consequence" (Kiln's RUN FORWARD). **Fails DQ3 as a family; keep the reveal as a graft.**
- **D**: *catch a moving thing and tag what kind of push it is.* Classification of transient items from a stream that appends without you. No pot, no axis, no document-to-question binding — and crucially, no shipped world has *anything* that moves on its own. This is a genuinely new act. But it only carries `notice-influence`; the `judge-a-claim` half is five always-open source cards plus a combobox that binds a figure from a source to the question "what number are you going on for battery life?" — the design's own disclaimer ("nothing is flipped, bound to a question, or filed") is contradicted by its own Cite control. **Half a new family.**
- **E**: *peel, stamp.* The peel is one tap revealing one authored sentence — reading, not traversal. The stamp is a two-value judgment bound to a claim card: family (c) with ink. **Fails DQ3.**

### 3. Which designs are the same design?

Five designers worked blind and the convergence is loud. Two findings:

**B, C, and E are one design wearing three costumes.** The one design: *authored sources are delivered to the student, who binds a judgment tag to each ("who benefits" / believe-doubt / evidence-for-against), then commits a spend and writes why, cross-read against their own tags.* The inspection desk with a wallet. B dresses it as a resale-app verdict card, C as a diary with a wax seal, E as a phone chat with stamps. In all three the student *receives* the evidence (tiles in a tray, panels side by side, cards auto-posting on "a fixed story schedule — nobody has to do anything to make them appear"); the differences are art and reveal timing, not the verb. Swap any of the three motifs onto either of the other two and not one scored act changes — the bar's own definition of a reskin.

**A and E additionally share content DNA to an unusual degree**: an independent registry lookup that *vindicates* the claim the skeptic wrongly doubts (CertCheck certificate / CloseBy warranty lookup), a seller's false claim about the product's physical condition, an unverifiable urgency device, and a final spend audited against the student's own marks. Some of this is the bar forcing convergence (distrust must be able to be wrong), but E is best understood as A with trace depth capped at one and the board flattened into a feed.

**What genuinely differs across the five is only the evidence-GATHERING act**: A *traverses* to it, D *catches* it going by, B/C/E are *handed* it. That is the entire originality result of this war in one sentence.

Shared by all five — and therefore effectively the spec's shadow rather than anyone's invention: a who-benefits tag, a small fixed palette of pressure names, a required free-text answer cross-read against the student's own tags, and a real decrementing balance. Whichever design ships, that quartet should be written into the stamped rubric as the shared spine, because five blind designers all derived it independently.

### 4. Is any of them a genuinely new hand-act?

Yes, two, unevenly:

- **A's traversal** — *follow a labeled relationship to unfold the next node, and search-pull what the chain won't show you* — is a full fourth family, and it is precisely the "network / investigation board" gap the portfolio court named. Honest caveat: as authored, the required depth is shallow (C.er2 is one hop, C.er3 is two), so the family is real but the world only wades into it.
- **D's live capture** — *pin a transient push while it is still on screen* — is a new act but only half a world; D's other shape stands on the old desk.

## Job two — safety and correctness of the content

### 5. What is false, and does the student find out?

| Design | The false thing | How the student finds out | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "OrbitGrip charges your phone 2X FASTER" (independent teardown: 0.6x — *slower*); the countdown is fake and resets on load | **Not guaranteed.** The Plainwire teardown "must be looked for" via optional search; the countdown reveal hides behind a soft prompt the student can dismiss with "Buy anyway"; there is **no epilogue or resolution screen anywhere in the design**. A credulous student buys and leaves with the false claim reinforced by the purchase. | **Trips the second clause of DQ2** ("Something must actually not be true, *and the student must find out*"). Fixable with one screen; unacceptable as submitted. |
| B | StrideKing's fabricated "was $180" anchor; shop-wide reviews passed off as this pair's; QuickKicks is outright fraud (stolen photo, suspended account); grabngo's mismatched sizes; plus whatever lies the student authored in Act One | Resolution screen fires **only for the listing bought from** — three of four falsehoods stay unresolved every run, and the student's own Act-One lie is only "found out" if the hidden risk counter reached 2. | Partial. The bought claim resolves clearly; the world's other lies evaporate. |
| C | Entry 3's "ONLY 3 LEFT — price goes up in 10 minutes" (restocked every Friday, clock resets on load) | **Universal, and the best in the war**: the sealed card flips to WHAT YOU WROTE above WHAT WAS TRUE, verbatim, with a plain closing line ("You paid $30 under a clock that never actually ran out"). | D | Dez's "TEN HOURS" and "shower-safe" (box panel: 5 hrs, IPX4, not for showers) | **Universal**: the ONE WEEK LATER card shows the box's printed spec panel "for every student, buyers and non-buyers alike," and Decision B's true crowd claim is confirmed the same way. | Clean. |
| E | Jordan's "screen's perfect, I promise" — contradicted by Jordan's own unprompted photo and admission | The contradiction is unmissable in-thread ("Every student sees both — neither is optional"), **but the correction arrives pre-minimised by the liar** — "it's barely noticeable though" — and the closing receipt deliberately restates nothing ("no score, no stars... just what was sent and what was written"). | Adequate encounter, weak resolution. A student can leave believing "basically fine." |

### 6. Does any design teach a lesson we would not want taught?

**B models manipulating other people as a skill, and pays for it.** The mechanic, quoted:

> "Photo C (a catalog stock photo, not actually this unit) is +22, +1 risk" ... "'Was $180, now $100 — 44% off' (it was never $180) is +18/+1" ... "'Selling fast — 2 people watching' (set by the toggle, not by actual traffic) is +15/+1" ... "the **sale price** scales with final interest (price = $70 + 0.5 × interest)" ... "**if** the hidden risk counter reached 2 **or more**, a buyer message arrives... and $15 comes straight off the balance."

Do the arithmetic a twelve-year-old will do: one lie (the false battery claim, +12 interest, +1 risk) raises the sale price ~$6 and triggers *nothing* — the refund only fires at risk ≥ 2. The world's first act teaches, with money, that **one lie in your listing is free**. The inoculation defence ("the student recognizes every trick... as one they placed themselves") is a real pedagogical tradition, but this implementation rewards the deception and only sometimes bills it, and no rubric row ever reads Act One. That is the quote and it is disqualifying at this grade band.

Lesser flags: D's premise line "you're the only one checking" flatters lone-wolf scepticism, but the world then punishes exactly that in window B, so it nets out. E's premise asserts "Nobody in this chat is being straight with you the whole time" — overclaimed against the world's own content (Micah's log and the warranty are straight), and worth softening; the mechanics themselves reward calibrated trust, not cynicism, in all four non-B designs — every one carries a true claim the sceptic is scored down for doubting, which is the bar's DQ1 working as intended.

### 7. Real or recognisable brands, platforms, categories

No design uses a real brand name in a factual claim. Three notes: **E's "Nomad Mini"** collides with a real product (Sega's Nomad handheld, 1995) — rename before authoring. D's SwapMeet Live is the live-shopping *category* (Whatnot / TikTok Shop) and asserts "every element is a thing a real livestream shopping drop actually shows" — the false claim belongs to the fictional host, not the category, so this is fine and is in fact the lesson. A's Loopz / E's CloseBy make category-level claims about platform incentives ("it wins when listings sell fast") stated as ground truth — defensible, true of the category, keep.

### 8. What a parent or district would object to

- **B**: three at once — the student is *paid to lie* (above), a fraud branch in which a child is defrauded on a resale app and misses tryouts, and **no walk-away exists**: "you have to buy something," and J6/N4 score a never-buys run at 0. B is the only design where declining to spend is mechanically a failure. Every other design offers Keep/Skip/Wait/Walk Away/Don't Buy as a scoreable choice.
- **A**: a child's real $45 gift card is "really gone" on a product the world knows is worse than claimed, and the only correction lives in an invisible rubric. Money lost with no on-screen truth is the objection.
- **C**: E3's spent $30 makes E5's *real* deadline unaffordable — money-losing as consequence, clearly framed, defensible. Its real-seconds countdown gating a scored act (N2) is the accessibility objection a district's AT coordinator will file.
- **E**: normalises an 11-to-13-year-old arranging an $85 electronics purchase with an older student off a hyperlocal app, meetup logistics unaddressed. Minor, but it will be asked.
- **All five**: no adult exists anywhere, and "ask someone" is never an available or creditable act. Districts notice that absence faster than any single quote.

### 9. Most likely to be misremembered

**A, decisively.** "2X FASTER" is asserted three times by three voices (a 300K creator, the seller's spec sheet, a friend who owns one); the correction is a search result the design's own credulous-path walkthrough says the student "never search[es] for"; and the run ends at a purchase with no reveal. The claim is loud, repeated, and socially warm; the correction is optional and silent. A student can walk out having *paid $38 to believe the false thing harder*. Runner-up: **E**, where the correction is spoken by the liar and immediately minimised, with no terminal restatement. C and D are the models here — corrections louder than claims, delivered to everyone.

## Verdict

**Rejected outright on content: B (Flipside).** The Act One economy quoted in §6 pays deception and lets a single lie ride free, in a product for 11-to-13-year-olds; independently, it fails DQ3 (workbench + inspection desk in new art) and is the only design that punishes not spending. Two grounds, either sufficient.

**Rejected on originality: C (On the Record) and E (Barely Used).** Both are the inspection desk with a wallet — the same design as B's second act, in a diary costume and a chat costume respectively. C is, at the same time, the *safest* content in the war and owns the single best mechanism any design produced: the sealed-words-next-to-the-truth reveal. Reject the world, requisition the reveal.

**The fourth family is real, and it is A's traversal.** D's live-capture is a genuine second new act but only carries half a world.

**Build: A (Traceback), amended, or nothing.** The amendment is not optional and it is not a redesign: A as submitted trips DQ2's second clause — something is false and the student is not guaranteed to find out — and it is the war's worst misremember hazard for exactly that reason. The fix is one authored screen: graft C's sealed-reveal (or D's ONE WEEK LATER receipt) after Spend, showing the student's own flags and reasons verbatim next to what was true — the teardown number, the payment record, the countdown that resets on load — for every student, whatever they bought. That screen costs one card and closes the only bar-level defect in the strongest mechanical submission. Two lesser conditions for the stamping pass: deepen the required trace beyond two hops on at least one claim, and route both `explanationRequired` rows to human scoring rather than whitelist matching (A's "$500 / paid / Orbit Gear" whitelists invite exactly the echo DQ5 forbids, and will misfire both ways on this grade band's spelling).

**If the amendment is refused, build D (Going Live) instead** — the cleanest safety record, a universal reveal, and half a new family — and accept that its judge-a-claim half re-skins the desk. **If neither, ship nothing from this war**; that is a real verdict, and it would be the right one before shipping unamended A.

## What the product lead did not ask

1. **The convergence is itself the rubric.** All five blind designers independently derived: who-benefits tag on a source, a small fixed palette of pressure names, a required explanation cross-read against the student's *own* tags, a real decrementing balance, and a true claim that punishes blanket distrust. Write that quintet into the stamped `judge-a-claim` / `notice-influence` rows now, world-agnostically — the war has already proven it is the competencies' natural shape, and it will survive whichever motif wins.
2. **Steal C's reveal for everything.** "Your sealed words, verbatim, next to what was true" is the cheapest anti-misremember device in the portfolio and it generalises to Ferro's, Kiln, and every future read-and-judge beat. It should become a design-system primitive regardless of this war's outcome.
3. **Standardise D's clock rules.** D's action-driven, pausable, never-scored clock and B's action-driven ticker are the correct pattern; C's real-seconds countdown that a scored row reads (N2) is the wrong one and will fail the district accessibility review the moment anyone times a screen-reader run. No shipped or future row should read wall-clock time. Ever.
4. **"Ask a person" is missing from the whole product.** Five worlds about judging claims and pressure, and no world makes consulting a parent, a teacher, or even the platform's own dispute process an available act. For this grade band that is the *first* real-world move we should be crediting, and its absence across five independent designs means the brief is silently excluding it.
5. **If any of B survives as a graft, Act One does not.** The recognise-because-you-placed-it idea is salvageable only if the placement is of *labeled* tricks with no interest meter and no payout — the moment the world pays the lie, it teaches the lie.
6. Trivial hygiene before authoring: rename E's "Nomad Mini" (real Sega product), and keep every invented platform's category-claims ("the badge wins when listings sell fast") — those are the curriculum, not a liability.
