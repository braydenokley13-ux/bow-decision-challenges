## Q1 — Do two motifs actually reach different students?

**The picker is real.** A student in a *Students pick* class meets
*"Pick a world. Make it count."* with two genuine cards before anything else
(`14a-picker.png`). Both name what the student is (*"Avery plays. You handle the money"* /
*"Mo does the cooking. You handle the money"*) and how long it takes.

**Finding Q1-A — a teacher cannot assign the pop-up to a whole class.** On the class-creation
form the second option reads literally *"One for everyone · Eight Weeks to the Showcase."*
There is no third radio and no world selector: `src/educator/MyClasses.tsx:231-234` renders
`WORLD_REGISTRY[DEFAULT_WORLD_ID]?.title`, and `DEFAULT_WORLD_ID` is hard-coded to
`"basketball"` (`src/domain/scenario/registry.ts:182`). So the teacher's real choice is
**"students pick" or "basketball"**. A 6th-grade teacher who knows her room and wants everybody
on the night market — the more accessible of the two, and the one with the shorter run — cannot
have it. Receipt: `07a-one-for-everyone-selected.png`.

**Finding Q1-B — a student who cares about neither gets nothing.** Both worlds are
earn-and-spend with a single protagonist whose money you hold. There is no household budget, no
club treasury, no part-time job, and no plain-numbers route. The product's own decision record
(`gauntlet/decisions/02-the-third-world.md`) says so, and the guide does not overclaim — but for
a district the honest reading is *two flavours*, not *reaches students with different interests*.

**Genuinely good.** The reading-help panel is shipped, not in flight: every screen carries
*Reading help* → *Read this screen* / *Words* / *Read every screen to me*, and *Words* is a
screen-scoped glossary that defines the words on that screen in one line each — *"sublet: a
place rented from somebody who is renting it themselves"* (`16b-reading-help.png`,
`17a-words-glossary.png`). For a district with D26's language profile this is the single most
useful accessibility feature in the product, and `D26_ANSWERS.md` understates it as **IN FLIGHT**.

## Q2 — Do analyst feedback and the final report surface the concepts?

**In-run feedback is the strongest thing in the product, and it names concepts rather than
scoring them.** Verbatim, from my own run:

- Wrong housing order → *"One of these prices is for the whole eight weeks, not for one week.
  Work out what each place costs across all 8 of them."* (`16a-wrong-order.png`)
- $225 typed for an eight-week total → *"Too low. Check that you counted every amount."*
  (`20a-wrong-total.png`)
- Bonuses added into the certain-income box → *"Too high. Only two payments are certain. A bonus
  has gone into this total."* (`24a-countOn-wrong.png`)
- Every tile tapped on the Week-5 change board → *"Too high. Something in your total was already
  promised before Week 5, so it did not change."* (`39a-week5-wrong.png`)
- Money parked where it buys nothing → *"Every $150 spent on rides buys back 1 hour a week.
  There are only 1 hour to buy, so past $150 more money buys nothing."* (`32a-plan-filled.png`)
- Counting both bonuses, the moment the plan board opens → *"$1,800 of this might not arrive. If
  neither bonus comes, Avery has $1,600 to decide."* (`31a-plan-board.png`)

That last one is the whole design working: the consequence of treating conditional money as
certain is shown as a number in the student's own plan, on the screen where they are about to
spend it, before anything punishes them for it.

**The Week-8 resolution reads the student's own decisions back with what each one did**, tagged
COST YOU / PAID OFF, including the closed-set reason they gave: *"Avery had told the coach they
were on the bus, and was not on it. $120 went on the team shoes instead. **You said nobody was
counting on it.**"* (`45a-shortfall-saved.png`). That is a formative moment a teacher cannot
easily manufacture.

**The student read-back exists and is shipped** at `/run/:class/:session` — six world-neutral
topics, each naming the idea (*Conditional money*, *Required costs*, *Pay yourself first*, *A
plan that balances*, *Needs, wants and goals*, *Committed money*) and stating what this student
did, with no score anywhere (`51a-student-run-report.png`). `D26_ANSWERS.md` calls this IN
FLIGHT; it is live.

**Finding Q2-A (moderate) — the read-back tells the student what they did, not what went wrong.**
My run failed its last safety check: asked to show the plan works without the $800 attendance
bonus, I was $800 short and saved it anyway using *"Save it, $800 still missing."* That is the
single most important thing that student got wrong, and the read-back never says it. What it
says is: *"Week 5 asked you about the $800 attendance bonus again, and you were still counting
it in."* True, neutral, and not the same sentence as *"and when you were asked to show the plan
worked without it, it did not."* A district asking "does a student learn what they got wrong and
why" gets: they learn what they did, in the right vocabulary, and they learn the concept name —
they do not learn which of their decisions failed.

**Finding Q2-B (low, but it is in the teacher's report and in the answer document) —
`1 time(s)` still ships.** `src/domain/recap/log.ts:44` carries a comment reading
*"`1 time` / `2 times` — the defect District 26 found shipping as `1 time(s)`, closed here"* and
exports a `times()` helper. The two places that actually build the teacher-facing sentence still
do not use it:

- `src/domain/evidence/observe.ts:144` — ``Committed money was reached for ${e.lockedMoveAttempts} time(s), then the repair was made from adjustable money.``
- `src/domain/scenario/worlds/food-truck/observer.ts:471` — ``Money already committed was reached for ${board.lockedMoveAttempts} time(s), and the repair was then made out of the lines that could still move.``

`gauntlet/D26_ANSWERS.md` quotes the second of these verbatim, `1 time(s)` and all, as its
example of *"what a teacher actually reads."* A comment saying a defect is closed, over a defect
that is open, in the sentence the answer document showcases, is worth more than its cosmetic
weight.

## Q3 — How do students explain their reasoning?

**Twice, and both moments are real.**

- **In the moment.** Week 3 hands Avery $150 in cash that three things want. After choosing, the
  student must answer *"What made you leave the away-game travel share and the present for
  Avery's sister out?"* from four closed options — *It was the one I only wanted / Nobody else
  was counting on it / It can wait, even if it costs more / It was the cheapest one to drop*
  (`36a-claim-why.png`). This is comparable across a class and it is *wrong-answerable* in an
  interesting way: one of the three claims is a promise already made to the coach, so
  "nobody else was counting on it" is a defensible-sounding reason that the Week-8 resolution
  later contradicts with the student's own decision. That is a share-out waiting to happen.
- **At the end.** Free writing against sentence starters, with a checklist gate.

**The gate is honest about what it checks, and it is checkable.** It asks for three things and
says which of them you have not done yet: *"✓ 2 of Avery's numbers, tapped. ◦ Write each number
you tapped into your answer. $6,300 and $400 are not in it yet. ◦ Write 2 sentences or more,
each with something in it. None yet."* (`46a-gate-garbage.png`).

**Finding Q3-A (accepted, not a defect) — the gate passes nonsense.**
`aaaa aaaa $6,300. bbbb bbbb $400.` clears it. That is the stated design: the product refuses to
let software judge a child's writing, and tells the student *"A person reads the writing, not
software"* — which I verified is true, not marketing. The cost lands on the teacher, and it is a
real cost in Q5's arithmetic, not a bug.

**The draft survives a reload.** I typed 399 characters, reloaded the page, and got the text and
the tapped figures back with the checklist still green (`48a-before-reload.png`,
`48b-after-reload.png`). Round 1's worst finding — the write-up not persisting — is closed on
same-device reload. Cross-device is tested below.

## Privacy and security as a vendor review would see it

**No compliance claim found anywhere in the product.** I searched the whole of `src/`, `server/`,
`api/`, `index.html` and `docs/` for FERPA, COPPA, "Ed Law", "§2-d", NYCPS, "compliant",
"complies", "approved by". Every hit is a **source comment explaining an obligation the code is
trying to meet** — `server/vault.ts:14`, `server/index.ts:126`, `server/retention.ts:13`,
`src/platform/identity/types.ts:13` — and **not one of them reaches a rendered string**. There
are zero hits in any `.tsx`. The product does not tell a district it is compliant with anything.
That is the correct posture and it is worth saying out loud.

The nearest thing to a claim is on the standards surface, and it runs the other way:
*"NYSED has not reviewed or endorsed BOW"*, *"NYSED does not assess personal finance education,
and requires no assessment of it"*, and four of the five listed objectives are labelled
**BOW CANNOT SEE THIS ONE YET** (`02-educator-guide.png`).
