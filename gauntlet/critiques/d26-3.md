## What is genuinely good

I am a reviewer who was told to look for a reason to say no. These are the things I would say
yes to, without qualification.

- **The in-run feedback is the best thing in the product and it is not close.** Every wrong
  answer comes back naming the concept the student got wrong, in their words, about their
  numbers: *"Only two payments are certain. A bonus has gone into this total."* /
  *"Something in your total was already promised before Week 5, so it did not change."* /
  *"One of these prices is for the whole eight weeks, not for one week."* No score, no red X, no
  retry counter shown to the child.
- **The plan board tells you the cost of your own risk before you take it.** *"$1,800 of this
  might not arrive. If neither bonus comes, Avery has $1,600 to decide."* And when money is being
  wasted it says so: *"past $150 more money buys nothing."*
- **The teacher's evidence page is the most defensible assessment output I have seen from an
  edtech product.** Every judgement is a named requirement with the rule underneath it and the
  moment from that child's run that produced it — including judgements a human would struggle to
  make: *"They said that nobody else was counting on it, which is true of the present for
  Avery's sister — and just as true of the team shoes, which they did pay for, so it does not
  account for this choice."* Every one has an **I read this differently** overrule beside it.
- **Absences are not zeros, everywhere, including in the denominator.** *"A mark on this attempt
  is out of 11 … The 2 this run never asked are not in it: absences, not zeros."*
- **The product refuses to describe a class it cannot describe.** Under five results the class
  page, the debrief and the objective state all decline, and say why.
- **Reading help is real, on every screen, and free of tracking.** Read-this-screen, a
  screen-scoped one-line glossary, and read-every-screen — with the guide correctly warning that
  none of it is translation.
- **The join flow asks whose computer this is** and defaults to *shared*, which is the actual
  failure mode of a Chromebook cart, plus a one-press *"Sign the whole class out"* on the roster
  for when the trolley goes back.
- **Refusing to boot without an encryption key.** Most vendors log a warning. This one stops.

## Verdict

**GO WITH CONDITIONS** for a District 26 pilot, at one grade band, with a named teacher lead.

The conditions, in the order I would put them in a purchase order:

1. Rewrite the educator guide so it describes the product that shipped — cross-device resume and
   multi-day use, and what a mixed class actually returns. Remove "One sitting, one device" and
   "one answer about the class".
2. Complete the ending: say to the student, once, on the Week 8 screen or the read-back, what
   their own last check said about a plan that needed money that was not certain.
3. Publish a one-page data-practices note the school can forward to a family, with no compliance
   claim in it — just what is stored, for how long, who can delete it and how.
4. Set the class-creation form's world choice to offer either world to a whole class.
5. Fix `1 time(s)`, and render the committed-money reach on the teacher's page wherever in the
   run it happened.

**Single largest blocker: the product's own documentation contradicts the product's own tests on
two of District 26's five questions — a district cannot verify vendor claims at scale, and when
the guide is wrong about scheduling and about what a mixed class returns, every teacher who
follows it loses the lesson for a reason they cannot diagnose.**

## Q1 — Do two motifs actually reach different students?

**The picker is real.** A student in a *Students pick* class meets
*"Pick a world. Make it count."* with two genuine cards before anything else
(`14a-picker.png`). Both name what the student is (*"Avery plays. You handle the money"* /
*"Mo does the cooking. You handle the money"*) and how long it takes.

**Finding Q1-A — a teacher cannot assign the pop-up to a whole class.** On the class-creation
form the second option reads literally *"One for everyone · Eight Weeks to the Showcase."*
There is no third radio and no world selector: `src/educator/MyClasses.tsx:231-234` renders
`WORLD_REGISTRY[DEFAULT_WORLD_ID]?.title`, and `DEFAULT_WORLD_ID` is hard-coded to
`"basketball"` (`src/domain/scenario/registry.ts:182`). So the teacher's real choice is
**"students pick" or "basketball"**. A 6th-grade teacher who knows her room and wants everybody
on the night market — the more accessible of the two, and the one with the shorter run — cannot
have it. Receipt: `07a-one-for-everyone-selected.png`.

**They are not the same task repainted, and I want to be specific about that** because "two
reskins" would be the easy dismissal. The spine is shared on purpose — certain vs conditional
money, a required-cost total, a three-line plan, a mid-run shock, a repair, a competing-claims
moment, a written defence. But each world carries decisions the other cannot ask.
Basketball has a **time budget**: hours in Avery's week, bought back at $150 an hour, with a hard
ceiling past which more money buys nothing. The market has an **inventory decision against
demand** — how many trays to cook against a printed crowd, with the surplus going in the bin —
plus a **labour hire** (book Marisol or work the window alone), a **standing order across two
unequal nights**, and an **ex-ante contingency**: *"You said this line would give it back"*
versus *"You did not say where it would come from, so it came out of the cushion"*
(`src/domain/scenario/worlds/food-truck/scenario.ts:710-760`). A student who plays both meets
genuinely different money problems. Two is still two, but two is not one twice.

**Finding Q1-B — a student who cares about neither gets nothing.** Both worlds are
earn-and-spend with a single protagonist whose money you hold. There is no household budget, no
club treasury, no part-time job, and no plain-numbers route. The product's own decision record
(`gauntlet/decisions/02-the-third-world.md`) says so, and the guide does not overclaim — but for
a district the honest reading is *two flavours*, not *reaches students with different interests*.

**Finding Q1-C — the sentence a teacher reads while choosing "students pick" is not true.**
The radio's own explanation is *"Both stories collect the same evidence, so **the results pool
either way**"* (`src/educator/MyClasses.tsx:219-220`). The results do not pool. The class page
renders **one section per world, each with its own denominator** — the code comment above it
says so in as many words: *"A class where students chose differently was asked two different
sets of questions, and each set has its own denominator"*
(`src/educator/RealClassPages.tsx:777-782`, `analysis.ts:200-211`,
`worldParity.test.ts:19` — *"a claim about a room is made about the room that was asked"*).
And no section narrates itself as a class below **five** results
(`MINIMUM_ASSESSED_FOR_A_STATE = 5`, `src/domain/competency/objectiveState.ts:34`).

The consequence is concrete and lands on the teacher who took the product at its word: a class
of 24 that splits 20/4 gets one narratable world section and one that will not describe itself;
a 12:1:1 class of eight that splits 5/3 gets the same; and every share-out prompt, contrast pair
and distribution is per-world, so the teacher planning a discussion has two half-classes instead
of one class. **The per-world split is the right engineering.** The defect is that the one
sentence a teacher reads at the moment of the decision tells them the opposite, and nothing
warns them again later. The educator guide repeats it —
*"a class where students chose differently still produces **one answer about the class**"*
(`src/educator/EducatorPages.tsx:121`, and visible on screen in `02-educator-guide.png`) — and so
does `gauntlet/D26_ANSWERS.md`, which offers it as a property with a test behind it. The test
behind it (`src/educator/worldParity.test.ts`) asserts the opposite property.

**Genuinely good.** The reading-help panel is shipped, not in flight: every screen carries
*Reading help* → *Read this screen* / *Words* / *Read every screen to me*, and *Words* is a
screen-scoped glossary that defines the words on that screen in one line each — *"sublet: a
place rented from somebody who is renting it themselves"* (`16b-reading-help.png`,
`17a-words-glossary.png`). For a district with D26's language profile this is the single most
useful accessibility feature in the product, and `D26_ANSWERS.md` understates it as **IN FLIGHT**.

## Q2 — Do analyst feedback and the final report surface the concepts?

**In-run feedback is the strongest thing in the product, and it names concepts rather than
scoring them.** Verbatim, from my own run:

- Wrong housing order → *"One of these prices is for the whole eight weeks, not for one week.
  Work out what each place costs across all 8 of them."* (`16a-wrong-order.png`)
- $225 typed for an eight-week total → *"Too low. Check that you counted every amount."*
  (`20a-wrong-total.png`)
- Bonuses added into the certain-income box → *"Too high. Only two payments are certain. A bonus
  has gone into this total."* (`24a-countOn-wrong.png`)
- Every tile tapped on the Week-5 change board → *"Too high. Something in your total was already
  promised before Week 5, so it did not change."* (`39a-week5-wrong.png`)
- Money parked where it buys nothing → *"Every $150 spent on rides buys back 1 hour a week.
  There are only 1 hour to buy, so past $150 more money buys nothing."* (`32a-plan-filled.png`)
- Counting both bonuses, the moment the plan board opens → *"$1,800 of this might not arrive. If
  neither bonus comes, Avery has $1,600 to decide."* (`31a-plan-board.png`)

That last one is the whole design working: the consequence of treating conditional money as
certain is shown as a number in the student's own plan, on the screen where they are about to
spend it, before anything punishes them for it.

**The Week-8 resolution reads the student's own decisions back with what each one did**, tagged
COST YOU / PAID OFF, including the closed-set reason they gave: *"Avery had told the coach they
were on the bus, and was not on it. $120 went on the team shoes instead. **You said nobody was
counting on it.**"* (`45a-shortfall-saved.png`). That is a formative moment a teacher cannot
easily manufacture.

**The student read-back exists and is shipped** at `/run/:class/:session` — six world-neutral
topics, each naming the idea (*Conditional money*, *Required costs*, *Pay yourself first*, *A
plan that balances*, *Needs, wants and goals*, *Committed money*) and stating what this student
did, with no score anywhere (`51a-student-run-report.png`). `D26_ANSWERS.md` calls this IN
FLIGHT; it is live.

**Finding Q2-A (the most important instructional finding in this review) — the run computes the
counterfactual that would teach the lesson, and then never shows it to anybody.**

My student did the thing the whole design exists to catch: built the opening plan on $1,800 of
conditional bonus money. The product handled that beautifully *in the moment* — the plan board
warned *"$1,800 of this might not arrive. If neither bonus comes, Avery has $1,600 to decide"*,
and Week 5 cancelled one of the two bonuses. Then, at the last check, the product asked her to
show the plan works without the remaining $800 and **she could not**: she was $800 short and
pressed *"Save it, $800 still missing"* — an escape hatch the product offers and records.

Three screens later, the Week 8 resolution tells her, under a green **PAID OFF** rule:

> **PAID OFF · Building the plan around the Perfect attendance bonus** — *"Avery made every
> session. The money you planned around actually landed."*

and, in a headline tile, **+$800 · "It arrived, and your plan was built on it."**
(`45a-shortfall-saved.png`). Nowhere on that screen, or on the student's read-back, or on the
teacher's evidence page, does the sentence *"and if it had not arrived you were $800 short — you
saved that plan yourself"* appear. The product knows the number. It asked for it. It stored the
acknowledgement.

To be fair to the design: this is **not** a rigged outcome. The bonus lands because she bought
back enough hours, and a student who does not buy hours loses it — the run can and does punish
the bet. And the preference-neutrality principle is right: counting conditional money in is not
a wrong answer, only an unhedged plan is. But a twelve-year-old reads a green **PAID OFF** beside
*"building the plan around the bonus"* as a verdict on the decision, and the one screen that
would complete the lesson — *your own last check said this plan needed money that was not
certain* — is the one the product does not draw. This is the single largest gap between what
the product observes and what a student learns from it.

**Finding Q2-B (moderate) — the read-back tells the student what they did, not what went wrong.**
My run failed its last safety check: asked to show the plan works without the $800 attendance
bonus, I was $800 short and saved it anyway using *"Save it, $800 still missing."* That is the
single most important thing that student got wrong, and the read-back never says it. What it
says is: *"Week 5 asked you about the $800 attendance bonus again, and you were still counting
it in."* True, neutral, and not the same sentence as *"and when you were asked to show the plan
worked without it, it did not."* A district asking "does a student learn what they got wrong and
why" gets: they learn what they did, in the right vocabulary, and they learn the concept name —
they do not learn which of their decisions failed.

**Finding Q2-C (moderate, validity) — a reach for committed money reaches the student and not
the teacher.** During the Week-5 triage I deliberately tapped the locked *"Course seat, reserved
and paid"* card. The product answered *"Already promised. This one cannot move."*
(`41a-tap-committed.png`) and recorded it: the student's own read-back says *"You reached once
for money that was already committed. It could not come back, so the repair had to come out of
the rows that could still move."* (`51a-student-run-report.png`).

The teacher's evidence page for the same attempt — all four tabs, the full judgement list and the
step-by-step trail — never mentions it, and grades the requirement whose whole subject it is:

> **Uses only money that can still move** — *Repairs from categories that are still adjustable;
> does not try to reclaim committed money.* … **BOW: Right first time**

(`70e-student-evidence.png`, and the full page text in the receipts). The mechanism is
`observe.ts`: `C5.2` counts locked-move attempts in the **final** repair only
(`observe.ts:159`), and `C4.2` — the observation that carries the sentence *"Committed money was
reached for N time(s)"* (`observe.ts:137-146`) — is bound to `primaryC4`, which for a student
who counted conditional money in resolves to the **fallback** board rather than the Week-5 first
response (`observe.ts:76-81`). An attempt made on the triage board therefore falls between the
two and is never rendered. So `D26_ANSWERS.md`'s showcase quote — *"Money already committed was
reached for 1 time(s)"* — is a sentence I could not get the product to print, in the run I
specifically constructed to print it.

**Finding Q2-D (low, but it is in the teacher's report and in the answer document) —
`1 time(s)` still ships.** `src/domain/recap/log.ts:44` carries a comment reading
*"`1 time` / `2 times` — the defect District 26 found shipping as `1 time(s)`, closed here"* and
exports a `times()` helper. The two places that actually build the teacher-facing sentence still
do not use it:

- `src/domain/evidence/observe.ts:144` — ``Committed money was reached for ${e.lockedMoveAttempts} time(s), then the repair was made from adjustable money.``
- `src/domain/scenario/worlds/food-truck/observer.ts:471` — ``Money already committed was reached for ${board.lockedMoveAttempts} time(s), and the repair was then made out of the lines that could still move.``

`gauntlet/D26_ANSWERS.md` quotes the second of these verbatim, `1 time(s)` and all, as its
example of *"what a teacher actually reads."* A comment saying a defect is closed, over a defect
that is open, in the sentence the answer document showcases, is worth more than its cosmetic
weight.

## Q3 — How do students explain their reasoning?

**Twice, and both moments are real.**

- **In the moment.** Week 3 hands Avery $150 in cash that three things want. After choosing, the
  student must answer *"What made you leave the away-game travel share and the present for
  Avery's sister out?"* from four closed options — *It was the one I only wanted / Nobody else
  was counting on it / It can wait, even if it costs more / It was the cheapest one to drop*
  (`36a-claim-why.png`). This is comparable across a class and it is *wrong-answerable* in an
  interesting way: one of the three claims is a promise already made to the coach, so
  "nobody else was counting on it" is a defensible-sounding reason that the Week-8 resolution
  later contradicts with the student's own decision. That is a share-out waiting to happen.
- **At the end.** Free writing against sentence starters, with a checklist gate.

**The gate is honest about what it checks, and it is checkable.** It asks for three things and
says which of them you have not done yet: *"✓ 2 of Avery's numbers, tapped. ◦ Write each number
you tapped into your answer. $6,300 and $400 are not in it yet. ◦ Write 2 sentences or more,
each with something in it. None yet."* (`46a-gate-garbage.png`).

**Finding Q3-A (accepted, not a defect) — the gate passes nonsense.**
`aaaa aaaa $6,300. bbbb bbbb $400.` clears it. That is the stated design: the product refuses to
let software judge a child's writing, and tells the student *"A person reads the writing, not
software"* — which I verified is true, not marketing. The cost lands on the teacher, and it is a
real cost in Q5's arithmetic, not a bug.

**The draft survives a reload.** I typed 399 characters, reloaded the page, and got the text and
the tapped figures back with the checklist still green (`48a-before-reload.png`,
`48b-after-reload.png`). Round 1's worst finding — the write-up not persisting — is closed on
same-device reload. Cross-device is tested below.

## Q4 — Monitoring across a class, homework and multiple days

**Finding Q4-A (serious for adoption, and it is a documentation defect rather than a code one) —
the educator guide tells teachers not to do the thing the product was built to do.** The guide,
which is the only training surface, says **"One sitting, one device"** twice — in the page lead
(`src/educator/EducatorPages.tsx:153`) and again in the one-minute brief
(`:104`) — and the *Running it* steps say only *"Allow 20–28 minutes."* Receipt:
`02-educator-guide.png`, captured from the running product.

Meanwhile the product carries a whole cross-device resume subsystem built specifically for the
other case. `src/student/ResumeGate.tsx:22-27`: *"Tuesday's run, opened on Thursday, on a
different Chromebook. The product tells teachers this works — homework across three days is one
of the things a class is set."* The student home screen carries *"Carry on"* with the class code
attached so the run is fetched from the service rather than the machine
(`src/student/Home.tsx:191-195`). The live panel is built around *"who stopped on Tuesday and
not come back"* (`src/educator/RealClassPages.tsx:392`).

So the answer to District 26's fourth question is: **the product does it, and does not tell the
teacher it does it.** I searched every educator surface for "resume", "carry on", "second day",
"homework", "come back" — the only hits are code comments and the teacher-account copy. A
teacher who reads the guide and plans accordingly will run this in one period on one device,
which is exactly the case the guide describes and the wrong case for a room that has to split
across two days. That is a training cost the product creates and then hides.

**What actually works, and it is the biggest change since round 1.** I set this up and ran it.

- **Live panel — works.** With one student turned in and one mid-run, the class page renders
  *"RIGHT NOW / WHERE THE ROOM IS · Turned in 1 of 8 · Working right now 1 of 8 · Not started 6
  of 8"*, then names every student who has not started —
  *"Not started: Diego R., Fatima K., Jayden W., Leo M., Priya S. and Sam O."* — and lists the
  one who is working with the screen she is on and how long ago:
  *"Nia T. · The first plan · just now."* Receipt: `70b-class-live.png`. Every count carries its
  own denominator, and the page says *"1 turned in — individual work below. BOW does not
  describe a class from fewer than 5 runs."*
- **Cross-device resume — works, tested the way a child hits it.** Nia started in class on
  browser profile A, stopped inside the plan stage, and I closed the profile. On a **completely
  separate browser context with no shared storage** — a home laptop — she typed the class code
  and her card code and got *"You stopped at The first plan"* on her own home screen
  (`70c-nia-deviceB-home.png`), pressed **Carry on**, and landed on plan question 1 with the
  Teammate Share she had chosen on the other machine still in the money panel at −$1,000
  (`70d-nia-deviceB-resume.png`). This is round 1's flat "absent", answered.
- **What monitoring deliberately is not** holds up on inspection: a stage name and a
  last-touched time, no clickstream, no time-on-task, no idle metric. That is the right line for
  a district and it is drawn where the product says it is.

## Q5 — Share-out, feedback, grading

**Feedback — works end to end, and this is new.** I opened Amina's evidence page, wrote
*"You kept $400 back after Week 5 and that is the reason your plan held. Next time, try building
the first plan without the two bonuses in it and see what you would have had to decide,"* and
pressed **Send it**. The page confirmed *"Sent. They will see it next time they open BOW"*
(`70f-feedback-sent.png`). I then opened Amina's own session in her browser and the note was on
her home screen under **FROM YOUR TEACHER**, dated, above her run — and the run tile now read
*"Turned in 8/19/2026. Your teacher has written back."* (`70g-student-sees-feedback.png`). There
is also a **"Worth talking about in person — for your list, not theirs"** flag which is correctly
never shown to the student.

**Reading queue — works.** One paragraph at a time, unread first, previous/next, the student's
own writing in full, a link out to their evidence, four criteria — Workability 0–2, Protected
priority 0–2, Trade-off 0–2, Numerical evidence 0–4 — and *"You score the writing; nothing here
is machine-scored"* on the page (`70h-reading-queue.png`). *Save review* stays disabled until
all four are set, which is the right refusal.

**Share-out — works and is honest about small n.** *"Pick what the room sees … Nothing is shown
unless you choose it. Up to 5, in the order you want to take them — two that disagree first is
usually the way in,"* with a **Show names on the screen** toggle that defaults off so the room
sees Plan A and Plan B (`70k-share-out.png`). The debrief refuses to narrate the class under
five results and says why — *"Under 5 results, this opens on the plans below rather than on
anything about the class"* and *"One student ran Eight Weeks to the Showcase, so there is no
second plan to set beside it yet"* (`70l-debrief.png`). A product that declines to make up a
discussion out of one run is a product a teacher can trust in front of a class.

## Would District 26 deploy this?

### On Chromebooks, on a school network

**The 1024×600 pass is clean.** I loaded seven surfaces at Chromebook resolution and measured
`scrollWidth − clientWidth` on each. Every one was **0px**: front door, the join screen, the
educator guide, the class page, the reading queue, the share-out picker and the class list
(`70m-1024-*.png`, `70n-1024-*.png`). Nothing needs a horizontal scroll on the hardware D26
actually has.

**The network story is a district's best case.** No CDN, no third-party script, no font host, no
telemetry. `vercel.json` ships a real content-security policy — `default-src 'self'`,
`script-src 'self'`, `connect-src 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `object-src 'none'` — plus
HSTS, `nosniff` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, and the API sets `Cache-Control: no-store` so a
shared Chromebook's disk cache never holds a child's evidence. There is exactly one origin to
allow through a content filter. That is a five-minute conversation with a network team rather
than a change-control ticket.

**Self-hosting is genuinely supported and fails closed.** `npm run api` runs the same handler on
Node with a file store, binds loopback by default, and prints a warning if you open it wider
without a TLS terminator. It **refuses to start a class** without `BOW_STORE_KEY`, and
`GET /api/health` distinguishes `ok` / `fresh` / `mismatch` so a mistyped key after a restore
reads as *"BOW_STORE_KEY has changed… Nothing has been deleted — put the original key back"*
rather than as an empty store. I triggered that state by accident — pointing a fresh key at a
directory another key had written — and the health endpoint caught it and returned 503. That is
the behaviour a district wants and almost never gets.

### What training does a teacher need?

**Two things reduce it and one thing adds to it.**

Reduced: the guide is a genuine one-page quick start, not a brochure — six answers, six
prerequisite skills, five steps, and a *"Try it as a student"* button. And the sample class at
`/educator/demo` is the *same real pages* fed real submitted evidence, so a teacher who wants to
see what comes back does not learn a workflow that does not exist.

Added: **the guide describes a different product than the one that shipped.** It says "One
sitting, one device" over a cross-device resume system (Q4-A). It says a mixed class produces
"one answer about the class" over an analysis layer that deliberately refuses to (Q1-C). Both
are exactly the kind of thing a teacher discovers at 9:15 on a Tuesday in front of 28
twelve-year-olds. Fixing the guide is cheaper than any other item in this review and buys more.

The unavoidable training cost is the assessment vocabulary — *Right first time / Fixed it
themselves / Part of it / Never came up*, and the three-bucket requirement count. The product
defines all four on the page every time it uses them, which is better than a glossary in a PDF,
but a first-time reader still has to absorb that BOW's marks are not out of 100 and that
"Never came up" is not a zero. Budget one 30-minute session, not a day.

### What would a parent ask, and can the school answer it?

Three questions, and the school has two and a half answers.

1. **"What does this company know about my child?"** Answerable, and the answer is unusually
   good: BOW never asks a student for a name, an email address or a birthday, the student
   account is an id and a credential, and the only human-readable label is the one the teacher
   typed on their own class list (`src/platform/identity/types.ts:6-15`). The join screen says
   it to the child: *"BOW never asks for your email, your birthday, or anything about your real
   money."*
2. **"Was my child graded by a computer?"** Answerable and true: the structured observations are
   rule-based and traceable, no composite score leaves the product, and the written explanation
   is scored only by the teacher. The student is told *"A person reads the writing, not
   software"* and the teacher page repeats *"You score the writing; nothing here is
   machine-scored."*
3. **"Can you delete it?"** Answerable and executed: the class list carries **Erase** — *"it
   deletes the name and everything that student did, for a family who has asked you to"* — and
   retention is 120 days with an hourly sweep that `GET /api/health` reports on.

**The half-answer, and it is a real gap for a district:** there is **no page anybody can send a
parent to.** No privacy notice, no data-practices page, no "what BOW keeps" route — I checked
`src/App.tsx`; there is no such route. Every one of the good answers above lives either in a
sentence on a sign-in form, in a source comment, or in `README.md`, which is not a document a
principal forwards to a family. D26 would have to write that page itself before the first
newsletter goes out, and it should not have to.

## Privacy and security as a vendor review would see it

**No compliance claim found anywhere in the product.** I searched the whole of `src/`, `server/`,
`api/`, `index.html` and `docs/` for FERPA, COPPA, "Ed Law", "§2-d", NYCPS, "compliant",
"complies", "approved by". Every hit is a **source comment explaining an obligation the code is
trying to meet** — `server/vault.ts:14`, `server/index.ts:126`, `server/retention.ts:13`,
`src/platform/identity/types.ts:13` — and **not one of them reaches a rendered string**. There
are zero hits in any `.tsx`. The product does not tell a district it is compliant with anything.
That is the correct posture and it is worth saying out loud.

The nearest thing to a claim is on the standards surface, and it runs the other way:
*"NYSED has not reviewed or endorsed BOW"*, *"NYSED does not assess personal finance education,
and requires no assessment of it"*, and four of the five listed objectives are labelled
**BOW CANNOT SEE THIS ONE YET** (`02-educator-guide.png`).

**Where the posture is stronger than most vendors D26 sees.**

- **The store refuses to run without encryption.** `BOW_STORE_KEY` is not a setting; a durable
  store that has not been given one returns `classroomReady: false` and will not open a class
  (`server/store.ts:929-940`, `server/vault.ts`). AES-256-GCM per record, fresh IV, tag beside
  it, and the vault **refuses an unsealed record** so an attacker who can write one file cannot
  swap in a plaintext teacher record and sign in.
- **The session-signing secret is derived from that key rather than stored**, so it exists in
  memory and in the operator's secret manager and nowhere on disk.
- **Key rotation exists and no longer leaks.** `npm run rekey` takes both keys from the
  environment, a key file or stdin, and explicitly refuses `--old-key`/`--new-key` on argv with
  a message saying why (`server/rekey.ts:306-310`). That closes a finding a previous security
  round reproduced live.
- **Retention is executed, not asserted.** 120 days, an hourly sweep, and `GET /api/health`
  reports `lastSweepAt` and `lastSweepDeleted`. I watched mine tick over.
- **Per-child erasure exists as a teacher control**, described in the words a teacher would use
  it in: *"it deletes the name and everything that student did, for a family who has asked you
  to… it cannot be undone"* — and separated from the reversible *take off the list*
  (`10a-roster-cards.png`).
- **The proxy-trust bug is fixed correctly.** `BOW_TRUST_PROXY` is a hop count read from the
  right-hand end of `X-Forwarded-For` (`server/index.ts:141-149`), so a caller cannot rotate a
  self-authored leftmost entry to walk through every per-address limit.

**Two things a §2-d reviewer would still write down.**

1. **The teacher's private link carries a bearer key in the URL** (`?key=…`). It is not sent to
   the API as a query parameter — the client moves it to an `X-BOW-Teacher-Key` header, so it
   does not land in server logs — and `Referrer-Policy: same-origin` keeps it out of
   cross-origin referrers. But it is in the browser's address bar and history on a shared staff
   machine, and it is the kind of string teachers paste into shared documents. The product
   mitigates this correctly by making accounts the primary route (*"It is on your account too,
   so it is on any computer you sign in on"*), and a district should simply require account
   sign-in and treat the link as a fallback.
2. **`GET /api/classes/:code` answers 200 with the class label and its assignment to anybody
   holding a five-character code, with no key and with a wrong key.** I reproduced it with
   `curl`: `{"code":"EEN4D","label":"Period 3 · Grade 7 Math", …}`. No student names, no
   evidence, no teacher key — the record is stripped (`server/handler.ts:589-590`) — and the
   join screen genuinely needs it so a child can confirm they typed the right code. The exposure
   is the label a teacher chose, which is often a period and a room. Worth one line in a data
   inventory, not a blocker.

**What I did not re-test.** Five previous security rounds have gone over the store, the rekey
tool, path traversal, rate limiting and the identity routes in far more depth than an adoption
review should duplicate. I read their conclusions, spot-checked the two findings the README
claims are closed (keys-on-argv, migration door) and found both closed in the source.
