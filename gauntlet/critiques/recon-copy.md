# Copy Critique — BOW Decision Challenges

Scope: `src/content/studentCopy.ts`, `src/educator/labels.ts`, both worlds' `scenario.ts`, every `.tsx` under `src/stages`, `src/educator`, `src/components`, `src/app`, plus 126 screenshots in `gauntlet/screens/{baseline,lead-teacher,lead-popup}`.

## SUMMARY

This is an unusually disciplined copy base. There is an automated ban-list test (`src/content/studentLanguage.test.ts`) that scans every student-facing string for assessment vocabulary and worksheet tone, and it passes — I could not find a single leaked "objective/skill/score/grade/great job/let's" in anything a student reads, including the `.tsx` files the test doesn't cover. Sentence-length is almost uniformly tight; the code comments articulate real editorial rules and mostly follow them. Grading this against a normal ed-tech product would be a short report.

Graded against its own stated bar, two things break it:

1. **The world picker tells the student they are being measured**, in the exact words the food-truck world's own header comment says a student must never be told. This is the single highest-visibility line in the product (first screen with a choice on it) and it is the clearest violation of the product's own rule I found anywhere.
2. **The Pop-Up world promises a written reply from a person that the product cannot deliver.** The teacher's reading tool only assigns numbers (0–2, 0–4 per rubric row); there is no field for a note, no delivery mechanism, and no place the student would ever see one. Basketball's parallel screen makes no such promise — so this is also a voice-consistency failure between the two worlds, not just an isolated overclaim.

Below those two, the real cost center is **vocabulary sprawl on the teacher side**: the product has at least five overlapping classification systems (concept, competency, objective, micro-skill, evidence requirement) and at least three overlapping vocabularies for "how well/how independently" (Independent / Independently / Demonstrated independently; With support / Corrected it / Demonstrated with support; etc.), all visible within a two-or-three-click radius of each other. Individually each vocabulary is well-reasoned in its own file's comments. Collectively, a teacher moving from the class dashboard to a single student's evidence trail crosses vocabularies without warning.

Student-side financial vocabulary is clean — no unexplained jargon found (see FINDINGS, item 12).

## STUDENT VOICE FINDINGS

- The two worlds are written in genuinely distinct, well-held voices (a coach/roster register for basketball, a market-organiser register for the food truck) and neither voice slips into the other. This is a real strength — most products with two "flavors" let the tone bleed.
- The single voice break is the promise in Pop-Up's write-up screen (FINDINGS #2) — everywhere else, Pop-Up is as unsentimental and plain as its own header comment demands.
- No AI voice, no fake excitement, no condescension, no redundant instruction found in either world's `scenario.ts` or in `studentCopy.ts`. The automated ban-list test (`content/studentLanguage.test.ts`) is doing real work and its exception list is short and argued, as its own comment requires.
- Financial vocabulary is clean. "Deposit" and "rebate" are the only two words in food-truck copy that a teacher might call jargon, and both are explained by the sentence that carries them rather than defined abstractly ("Ramos will put the $300 deposit against it, and you have to find the difference" — the mechanic is stated, not just the word). No unexplained term reached a screen.
- One visual defect actively destroys readability of otherwise-good student copy: at the 1024px breakpoint, the Week 1–4 story cards (`gauntlet/screens/baseline/1024-08-season-weeks.png`) collapse into a column too narrow for their own text, wrapping "First practice Monday. Avery comes off the bench…" one or two words per line for roughly fifteen lines per card. The same content is perfectly readable at 1366px and at the narrow (640px, single-column) breakpoint — this is a mid-width CSS regression, not a wording problem, but it means real 12–14-year-olds on a standard 1024-wide school laptop screen cannot finish reading Avery's week without extreme scrolling and re-parsing.

## TEACHER VOICE FINDINGS

- The teacher surfaces are consistently scannable at the sentence level — most headings are five words or fewer, most body copy is one or two short sentences, data tables carry their own labels. This is good work and the exception below is genuinely the exception, not the rule.
- The one sentence that fails the two-second-scan bar badly is also the highest-stakes sentence in the teacher flow (FINDINGS #3): the one-time private-link warning on class creation.
- Every teacher page carries an explicit, honest disclaimer where a claim could otherwise overreach: "NYSED has not reviewed or endorsed BOW," "Mapped to NYSED objectives, not scored against them," "nothing here is inferred or AI-generated," "No points total for this world" (with the reason given in the same sentence). This is the product declining to overclaim in exactly the places a lesser product would. Worth preserving as a pattern.
- The five-taxonomy vocabulary problem (concept / competency / objective / skill / requirement — see table above) is the main thing standing between "teacher can scan this" and "teacher fully understands what they are looking at." None of the individual pages fail the two-second test; the failure is cross-page, when a teacher has to hold two of these five words in their head at once and guess whether they're the same thing.

## FINDINGS

### CRITICAL

**1. The world picker tells the student they are being measured — directly contradicting the product's own stated law.**
Quote: *"Your teacher wants to see that you can build a budget that works. Choose the challenge you want to try."*
File: `src/content/studentCopy.ts:48` (`STUDENT_COPY.choose.deck`), rendered in `src/stages/WorldChoice.tsx:101`. Confirmed on screen: `gauntlet/screens/lead-popup/02-world-choice.png`.
Why it's critical: the food-truck scenario file's own header comment states the rule this line breaks, in almost these exact terms: *"There is no word in here about how any of it will be read, because a student who is being told they are being measured stops making the decision and starts guessing the answer."* (`src/domain/scenario/worlds/food-truck/scenario.ts:14-16`). The automated ban-list test enforces the same principle by word-matching ("objective," "assess," "score"…), but this sentence achieves the same disclosure without tripping any banned word — it says "your teacher wants to see that you can" instead of "you are being assessed on," which is a paraphrase, not an escape from the rule. It is also the single most-seen line in the two-world build: every student who gets a choice reads it before touching anything else. The file's own comment (`studentCopy.ts:39-44`) already flags this as "the closest the student flow comes to naming the thing at all" — the authors knew they were pushing right up to the line. This sentence is over it.
Proposed replacement: cut the surveillance framing entirely and let the two cards do the selling, the way `choose.title` already does. *"Pick a world. Make it count."* is already the headline; the deck line doesn't need to add a reason. Replace with something that describes the choice, not its audience: *"Two stories, same kind of trouble: not enough certainty and not enough money. Pick the one you want to run."*

**2. Pop-Up promises a person will "write back" — a feature the product does not have — and breaks voice parity with Basketball in the process.**
Quote: *"A person reads this and writes back, so use your own numbers and say what you were thinking."*
File: `src/domain/scenario/worlds/food-truck/scenario.ts:360` (`writeUp.note`), rendered on the write-up screen via `src/stages/popup/PopUpScreens.tsx:750`.
Why it's critical: I traced the entire teacher-facing review path (`src/educator/ReadingQueue.tsx`). The only instrument a teacher has is four rubric buttons, each 0–2 or 0–4 points ("Workability," "Protected priority," "Tradeoff/opportunity cost," "Numerical evidence") and a "Save and read the next" button. There is no free-text field, no reply mechanism, no notification path, and nothing in any student screen (including `SubmittedStage` and Pop-Up's own `submitted.sent`) that ever displays a reply. A student who reads "a person… writes back" and later checks for a response will find nothing, because nothing was ever built to deliver one. Compare Basketball's parallel line at submission — *"A person reads the writing. Software can check whether the money adds up. It should not decide whether your thinking makes sense."* (`src/stages/StudentChallenge.tsx:1338-1340`) — which promises only reading, accurately. Pop-Up's own second mention, at submission, gets this right too (*"A person reads what you wrote… It should not decide whether your thinking makes sense,"* `food-truck/scenario.ts:564`). Only the pre-writing prompt overclaims, and it does so in the one world whose header comment is most explicit about never misleading the student.
Proposed replacement: match the accurate line already used twice elsewhere in the same file. *"A person reads this, not software, so use your own numbers and say what you were actually thinking."*

### HIGH

**3. The single highest-stakes teacher sentence in the product is a 40-word run-on, delivered exactly once.**
Quote: *"Bookmark this. It is saved in this browser, it is the only thing that opens this class's evidence, and it is not shown again — the class code alone will not open it, which is what stops students reading each other's work."*
File: `src/educator/MyClasses.tsx:235-237`. This renders on the class-creation confirmation screen, immediately under a code block containing the private link — the only copy of that link the teacher will ever see, per the code's own comment two lines above it ("Shown once, and kept in this browser. There is no account to recover it from, so the page says so…").
Why it matters: a sentence carrying four independent facts (bookmark it now / browser-scoped / never shown again / class code alone won't work) joined by two commas and a dash is not something a teacher skims in the two seconds they're likely to give it before clicking through to their new class. This is exactly the sentence where under-reading has a real, unrecoverable cost — a teacher who doesn't register "not shown again" loses access to a live class's evidence with no recovery path.
Proposed replacement, four short sentences instead of one long one: *"Bookmark this now — it will not be shown again. It only works in this browser. The class code alone won't open it, which is what keeps students out of each other's work."*

**4. Vocabulary sprawl: five overlapping "what a student can do" taxonomies and three-plus overlapping "how well" scales, visible within a few clicks of each other.**
See the TERMINOLOGY COLLISION TABLE above for the full evidence trail (concept/competency/objective/skill/requirement; Independent/Independently/Demonstrated independently). Concrete example of the cost: on `gauntlet/screens/baseline/1024-19-seat-14.png`, the same seat's page shows a "STATUS" column reading **Independent**, a "TRAJECTORY" column reading **Independent first opportunity**, and a banner reading **"Demonstrated independently"** — three different phrasings of the same fact, stacked in one screen. One click away, on the Evidence Trail tab (`1024-15d2-student-override.png`), the equivalent per-line judgement is picked from a six-button control reading **Independently / Corrected it / After a hint / Partly / Not demonstrated / Never came up** — a fourth vocabulary for the same axis, this time as the thing a teacher actively clicks to override.
Why it's high, not critical: none of these are wrong in isolation, each is well-reasoned in its own file's comments, and none use banned language. But the task given to a teacher — hold a mental model of what one student can do — is made measurably harder every time the word changes without the meaning changing.
Proposed direction (not a single line-edit — this is a structural fix): pick one adjective family for "how independently" (recommend the LEVEL_LABELS set — "Independently / Corrected it / After a hint / Partly / Not demonstrated / Never came up" — since it is the most concrete and least jargon-adjacent of the four) and route every other surface (`STATUS_LABELS`, the golden-evidence-case banner, the trajectory column) through it or through an explicit, visible "these two numbers are counting different things" caption. At minimum, add one sentence on the seat page explaining why "Independent" (status) and "Independent first opportunity" (trajectory) are not the same claim.

### MEDIUM

**5. A CSS breakpoint bug makes the Week 1–4 story unreadable at a common laptop width.**
Screens: `gauntlet/screens/baseline/1024-08-season-weeks.png` (broken — story text wraps to ~1 word per line across roughly 15 lines per card) vs. `1366-08-season-weeks.png` and `narrow-08-season-weeks.png` (both render correctly). Copy affected: the Week 1–4 narrative beats in `src/domain/scenario/worlds/basketball/scenario.ts:63-99` ("First practice Monday. Avery comes off the bench in the opener and does not turn the ball over once." etc.) — genuinely good, tight sentences that become functionally unreadable purely from layout at 1024px, a standard school Chromebook/laptop resolution. This is not a wording defect, but it fails the "a 12-year-old would not finish reading" bar as hard as any sentence in the product, because reading it requires fighting the column width rather than the prose.

**6. "Room" is a lone synonym for "class" in the Educator Guide's step list.**
Quote: *"Run the debrief with the room."*
File: `src/educator/EducatorPages.tsx:157`, step 5 of "Running it" on the Guide page (`gauntlet/screens/lead-teacher/09-guide.png`). Every neighboring step in the same five-item list, and every other page in the product, says "class." One word out of five breaks the pattern for no evident reason.
Proposed replacement: *"Run the debrief with the class."*

**7. Loading-state copy has a dangling object.**
Quote: *"Finding what your class was set…"*
File: `src/content/studentCopy.ts:53` (`STUDENT_COPY.choose.checking`). "Set" needs an object ("set to," "set up as," "set for you") — as written it reads like a sentence that lost its ending, not a considered ellipsis. It's a transient loading state so exposure is brief, but it's the first thing a student with the world-picker enabled sees while the page resolves what they're allowed to choose.
Proposed replacement: *"Finding what your class was set up for…"*

### LOW

**8. A missing comma turns one Pop-Up sentence into a momentary run-on.**
Quote: *"It is the most expensive one going, and on your own you cannot hand over more than 45 plates in an evening however many people are waiting."*
File: `src/domain/scenario/worlds/food-truck/scenario.ts:303` (Bridge Gate `tradeoff`), visible on `gauntlet/screens/lead-popup/04-spot.png`. "…in an evening however many…" reads as one clause until the eye catches up. Twenty-seven words is also the single longest sentence found anywhere in either world's copy.
Proposed replacement: *"It is the most expensive one going, and on your own you cannot hand over more than 45 plates in an evening, no matter how many people are waiting."*

**9. Assignment-flow heading briefly misreads as one run-on instruction.**
Quote: *"Set 1.3 Create a budget for a class you already have"*
File: `src/educator/MyClasses.tsx:294` (`<h2>Set {arriving.code} {arriving.shortLabel} for a class you already have</h2>`), visible on `gauntlet/screens/baseline/1024-15i-assign.png`. The injected objective code and label sit mid-sentence with no punctuation separating them from the surrounding words, so "Set 1.3 Create a budget for a class…" reads for a beat like a single run-on command rather than "Set [1.3 · Create a budget] for a class you already have."
Proposed replacement: add a separator the template can't lose — *"Set {arriving.code} · {arriving.shortLabel}, for a class you already have"* → *"Set 1.3 · Create a budget, for a class you already have."*

**10. Financial vocabulary check (student copy) — clean, listed for completeness.**
Only two words in student-facing copy edge toward finance jargon: **"deposit"** (food-truck generator repair: *"Ramos will put the $300 deposit against it,"* `food-truck/scenario.ts:347`) and **"rebate"** (*"Sell-out rebate,"* `food-truck/scenario.ts:313`). Neither is defined in the abstract, but both are demonstrated by the sentence that carries them (what the deposit buys back, what triggers the rebate and how much), which satisfies the file's own Rule 3 ("if two numbers relate, the copy says how"). Basketball never surfaces "deposit" to the student at all — the course-seat screen says "Reserve it now" / "Wait and decide later" instead, keeping the word entirely off-screen. No action needed; noted because the task asked for the full list.

**11. Gradebook asymmetry between worlds is disclosed, not hidden — noted, not a defect.**
Basketball seats show a numeric points line ("90 of 90 structured"); Pop-Up seats show "No points total for this world" with a one-sentence reason (`src/educator/RealClassPages.tsx:717-746`, code comment at `:708-715` explains why: the points spine is Basketball's own eighteen steps and would misreport a Pop-Up run as having "failed" work it never had a shot at). This is exactly the kind of claim the task asked me to check for overreach, and it holds up — the copy explains itself in place rather than leaving a teacher to wonder why one seat has a number and the other doesn't. Filed as a positive, not a finding requiring a fix.
