# Accessibility critique — a11y-3

**Question:** can every student in a class use this?
**Bar:** WCAG 2.2 AA, verified in a real browser rather than asserted, plus the harder question a
standard cannot ask — *is what a blind or keyboard-only student hears enough to do the task, or
only enough to know something happened?*

**Under test:** commit `8685d0b`, exported to a clean directory and served from there, so nothing
another agent edited mid-audit could move under a measurement.
**Where:** app on `http://127.0.0.1:5410` (Vite), class service on `http://127.0.0.1:5480`
(`BOW_CLASS_STORE=memory`, so nothing here touched another agent's classes).
**Browser:** Chromium 1194 at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`, driven by Playwright 1.62.1 with
`executablePath` pinned — nothing was installed. axe-core **4.13.0** from `node_modules`.
**Receipts:** `gauntlet/receipts/a11y-3/`. Every line quoted below is in one of those files, and
the drivers that produced them are in `gauntlet/receipts/a11y-3/drivers/`.

**What "keyboard-only" means here.** Nothing below was clicked. The driver presses `Tab`, reads
`document.activeElement` out of the page, and presses `Enter` only when the element it is
standing on is the one it wants. A control it cannot reach in 160 `Tab` presses fails the run.
Both complete student runs and the teacher's setup-to-grading pass were played that way.

## What is done well, and it is not a small list

A critique that opened with the defects would be a dishonest description of this product. Six
things here are better than most commercial ed-tech I have driven.

1. **axe is silent.** Sixty-seven distinct screens scanned against `wcag2a wcag2aa wcag21a
   wcag21aa wcag22aa`: **zero violations, zero failing nodes.** Not "no criticals" — none at all.
   `axe/basketball.json`, `axe/popup.json`, `axe/educator.json`. Nothing in this report is an axe
   finding, because there were none to report; everything below is something axe cannot see.
2. **Focus is managed on arrival at nearly every screen.** Fifty screen changes were recorded by
   reading `document.activeElement` rather than guessing. The world picker — a BLOCKER in the
   previous accessibility recon — now lands on `h1 "Pick a world. Make it count."` The stage
   heading is a real focus target, and stages that change their own question under one stage id
   move focus with `focusKey`, so *"Question 1 of 4"* → *"Question 2 of 4"* is announced.
3. **The announcement stream on the Basketball side is genuinely informative.** The money ledger
   speaks every change (`Still to give a job $3,100.` → `$1,900` → `Nothing left to place.`), and
   the writing gate announces its own three conditions and updates each as it is met:
   `✓ 2 of Avery's numbers, tapped. ◦ Write each number you tapped into your answer. $5,000 and
   $1,200 are not in it yet. ✓ 2 sentences so far.` A blind student can finish the written
   defence on what they hear. Fifty-one distinct announcements in one run:
   `screenreader/basketball-announcements.txt`.
4. **The reading tools cost the student nothing, and that is true on the bytes.** After opening
   the tools, opening the glossary and pressing read-aloud, every key this device holds was
   dumped: `bow.reading.v1` is a device-local preference, and the attempt log contains
   `["SESSION_STARTED","STAGE_ENTERED","WORLD_CONFIRMED","STAGE_ENTERED","SETUP_RANKED"]` — no
   reading event of any kind. Grepping the whole of `localStorage` for `READ_ALOUD`, `readAloud`,
   `glossary`, `speech` returns **zero**. The guide's claim to teachers is true.
   `probes/read-aloud.log`.
5. **The glossary is a correct modal dialog.** `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`, labelled by its
   own `h2`, focus moves to the heading on open, `Tab` is trapped inside (fourteen presses,
   three stops, nothing leaked), `Escape` closes it, and **focus returns to the "Words" button
   that opened it.** `probes/tools.log`. It also degrades honestly on a machine with no voice:
   pressing *Read this screen* on this container printed and announced *"This computer has no
   voice, so it cannot read out loud. Words still works."*
6. **Nothing in the student flow is timed.** No countdown, no auto-advance, and a shared-device
   student session lasts 10 hours (`SHARED_DEVICE_SESSION_HOURS = 10`) — longer than a school
   day. WCAG 2.2.1 is not engaged because there is no time limit to extend. A student who needs
   three times as long gets three times as long. **This is the single best thing about the
   product for the students this report is about**, and it is easy to miss because it is an
   absence.

One repair from the previous recon is also closed and worth naming: `onShowAndContinue` is now
passed at both `<PopUpBoard>` call sites (`PopUpScreens.tsx:569`, `:1002`), so the Pop-Up's
"stuck" ladder exists in both worlds.

## Findings, ranked by what actually stops a student

### BLOCKER 1 — Getting an arithmetic answer *right* produces silence and throws focus to `<body>`. Six times, in both worlds.

The product asks a student to work out a sum and press **Check**. When the answer is wrong, the
verdict is announced and focus stays on the button. When it is **right**, the whole
`CalculationInput` — the button, and the `aria-live` line that was about to say *"That's the full
amount."* — is unmounted in the same tick. The message never reaches an assistive technology, and
`document.activeElement` becomes `<body>`.

Reproduced at six separate sums:

| Screen | after pressing Check with the correct answer | receipt |
|---|---|---|
| Basketball · plan Question 1 | `focus = BODY (nothing focused)`, live regions: *(none on the page)* | `keyboard/basketball.log:155` |
| Basketball · plan Question 3 | next activation starts `from "BODY (nothing focused)"` | `keyboard/basketball.log:203` |
| Pop-Up · what you owe before you open | `focus = BODY (nothing focused)` | `keyboard/popup.log:41` |
| Pop-Up · what is left to plan with | `focus = BODY (nothing focused)` | `keyboard/popup.log:75` |
| Pop-Up · what the order costs | `focus = BODY (nothing focused)` | `keyboard/popup.log:135` |
| Pop-Up · what you still owe on the swap | `focus = BODY (nothing focused)` | `keyboard/popup.log:202` |

**Reproduce it by hand.** Join a class, open Basketball, rank the three places, choose the
cousin's room, type its eight-week total, press *Build the plan*. On *Question 1 of 4* type the
correct figure and press **Check** with the keyboard. Nothing is spoken; `document.activeElement`
in the console is `<body>`.

**What it costs a student.** A blind student presses the one button on the screen and hears
nothing at all. They cannot tell whether the answer was accepted, refused, or whether the press
registered — the three things that decide what they do next. The recovery is also not free: focus
is at the top of the document, so `Tab` restarts from the page's first control rather than from
where they were reading. Fifty-one announcements in that run, and none of them for the six
moments the product asks the student to be right about a number.

Two smaller pieces of the same defect, from `probes/student-probes.log`:

- **A repeated wrong answer is silent too.** Attempt 1 announced
  `[polite] Too low. Both payments count — the money already saved and the base pay.`
  Attempt 2, a different wrong number, announced **`[]`** — the verdict text had not changed, so
  the live region never mutated and nothing was spoken. A student hears a hint once and then
  presses Check into silence however many times they try.
- The verdict is `aria-describedby` on the input, which is right, and is why the *first* wrong
  answer works. That is the model the correct answer should have kept.

The whole finding is one rule: **the element that says what happened must outlive the press that
made it happen.**

### BLOCKER 2 — The hint ladder — the thing built for the students who are struggling most — is silent and drops focus, twice.

After two wrong answers the product offers *Show me one step*; after three, *Show the answer and
keep going*. Both are keyboard-reachable. Neither works for a screen reader.

```
after "Show me one step": focus = BODY (nothing focused)
  announced: []
  hint text now on screen: "Try it this way: Open “The four payments” at the top of the screen.
                            Add the two that arrive no matter what: $500 + $4,500."
  A11Y TREE around the hint:
    note
    button "Show the answer and keep going" [focusable=true]
```
```
after "Show the answer and keep going": focus = BODY (nothing focused)
  announced: []
```
— `probes/student-probes.log`, probe B

The hint renders into a `role="note"` that is **not** a live region and never takes focus. A
student presses the button that exists to unstick them, hears nothing, and is dropped at the top
of the document; the help they asked for is somewhere below, unannounced. The same is true of the
last rung, which supplies the answer outright.

**Reproduce.** Same route as BLOCKER 1. Type a wrong figure and press Check three times, then
press *Show me one step*. Read `document.activeElement`.

**Why this outranks a lot of prettier findings.** The two controls in this product that exist
specifically for a student who cannot do the task are the two that give that student the least.

### BLOCKER 3 — Pressing *Leave this run* puts focus nowhere, and the next `Tab` lands on the button that destroys the work.

```
after pressing "Leave this run": focus = BODY (nothing focused)
  announced: []
  buttons now: ["Yes — clear it and start again","No — keep working"]
  the next four Tab stops from wherever focus landed:
    1: button.button.button--danger "Yes — clear it and start again"
    2: button.button.button--quiet "No — keep working"
```
— `probes/tools.log`, probe D

The whole product teaches one motor pattern: `Tab` to the primary button, press `Enter`. On this
screen that pattern erases an unfinished run — *"Nothing here has been turned in yet. Leaving
clears this run off this computer, and it cannot be got back."* — and the sentence saying so is
never announced, because focus went to `<body>` instead of into the confirmation.

`Escape` does not close the run menu either (`run-menu[open]` count is still `1` afterwards), so
the only ways out are the two buttons, one of which is destructive and first.

**Reproduce.** In a run, `Tab` to the seat control in the top bar (`This run: XXXXX · seat 1`),
`Enter` to open it, `Tab` to *Leave this run*, `Enter`. Then `Tab` once and read where you are.

### MAJOR 4 — Basketball tells a child *"You worked this out."* after they pressed **Show the answer and keep going**

`probes/student-probes.log`, probe B, after the answer was supplied:

> **You worked this out.** Avery can count on $5,000 whatever happens on the court.

`studentCopy.ts:101–107` carries a second string for exactly this case — `shown: "Here is the
answer."` — with a comment saying the single string "used to fire either way" and that a
thirteen-year-old reads it as "the machine either not noticing or being sarcastic". The branch is
`floorShown = state.calculations["reliable-floor"]?.supplied === true`
(`StudentChallenge.tsx:757`), and **nothing in the Basketball reducer ever sets that flag**:
`SHOW_AND_CONTINUE_USED` writes only `state.support[...] = "answer_supplied"`
(`reducer.ts:293`), while `CALCULATION_SUBMITTED` copies `supplied: previous?.supplied ?? false`
(`reducer.ts:158`). The `shown` copy is unreachable in this world.

The Pop-Up's own machine does it correctly — `machine.ts:515–519` sets
`sums[id] = { ...sum, supplied: true }` — so the two worlds say different things to the same
child in the same situation.

The teacher's evidence is *not* wrong: the event carries `answer_supplied` and the support level
caps credit. Only the sentence the child reads is untrue. That is worse rather than better: the
one person being misled is the one the accommodation is for.

### MAJOR 6 — On the teacher's side, per-student controls and per-student headings carry no student

Three instances, all in `keyboard/educator.log`:

```
TAB RING — roster with cards (32 stops):
   2: button "Print a new card"    3: button "Take off the list"    4: button "Erase"
   5: button "Print a new card"    6: button "Take off the list"    7: button "Erase"
  ... six students, eighteen stops, three distinct accessible names
```

Nothing in any of those names says whose row it is. A teacher using a screen reader or voice
control cannot tell Ana's *Erase* from Pat's, and *Erase* is the destructive one. Same shape on
one student's evidence page: **fourteen** buttons all called *"I read this differently"*, one per
judgement, with nothing in the name saying which judgement.

And the class list is headed by status rather than by student. On the sample class:

```
H2  Every student who turned in
H3  Showed it
H3  Showed it
H3  Not yet
H3  Evidence not all in
H3  Showed it
... eighteen students, eighteen headings, four distinct strings, no names
```

Heading navigation is how a screen-reader user skims a list of eighteen. Here it produces
eighteen status words and no way to tell whose. The link text underneath *does* carry the name
(`a "Seeded Student 1 Not yet Savings is a planned amount — Did not do it"`), so the information
exists — it is the two structures a screen-reader user actually navigates with, headings and
control names, that are missing it.

### MAJOR 11 — At 320px, opening the reading tools hides the plan's commit button completely — a WCAG 2.2 · 2.4.11 failure

Same family as MAJOR 8 and the sharpest instance of it. With the reading tools **open** at 320×640,
the tab ring was walked and, at every stop, the focused element's rectangle was sampled at
twenty-five points to ask what is actually painted there:

```
--- the opening plan board — reading tools OPEN ---
  24. [ENTIRELY HIDDEN] button.button--primary "Check this plan" at 79,485 166x44
      — under aside.plan-rail — visible samples 0/25
```
— `probes/focus-obscured-w320.log`

**2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA**: when a component receives keyboard focus, no part of
it may be entirely hidden by author-created content. Twenty-five of twenty-five sample points on
the focused *Check this plan* button return the plan rail. The student can still press `Enter` —
activation does not care what is painted on top — but they cannot see where their focus is, on
the button that commits the run's biggest decision.

The cause is the accommodation's own layout rule. `reading.css` pushes `.plan-rail` up by
`--bow-reading-tools` when the tools are open, at `max-width: 980px`. That moves the fixed money
rail off the reading panel and onto the commit button underneath it. Turning the reading help on
is what does it.

Two more from the same walk, both partial rather than total, so 2.4.11 (Minimum) is not engaged
but a low-vision student still loses the focus ring:

```
   1. [covered 73%] button.ledger__toggle "See where the money goes" — under aside.reading-tools — visible 5/25
  12. [covered 39%] button "3 Money already owed"                    — under aside.plan-rail     — visible 15/25
```

At 1366×768 the same walk finds **nothing covered at all** (`probes/focus-obscured-desktop.log`),
so this is entirely a narrow-viewport defect — which is to say, a phone and a 400%-zoom defect.

### What holds under motion, colour and forced colours

All from `media/media.log`.

- **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is a real global kill switch, not a partial one.** Every
  declared transition on the plan board resolves to `1e-05s` and the `stage-in` entrance
  animation is gone. With no preference the same screen declares `stage-in 0.56s`,
  `transition:background 0.56s`, and eight others. Nothing has to be enumerated for this to keep
  working, which is why it does.
- **Measured contrast: nothing below its WCAG floor.** Computed off the real rendered colours
  with alpha composited against the first opaque ancestor, on the plan board: zero text runs
  under 4.5:1 (or 3:1 where large). axe's own `color-contrast` rule agrees across all 67 screens.
- **There is no dark mode.** `prefers-color-scheme: dark` leaves `body` at `rgb(240, 233, 219)`
  and the document at `color-scheme: light`. That is a defensible single-theme commitment rather
  than a bug — the declaration stops the browser force-inverting form controls into an
  inconsistent half-state — but it does mean a student who needs a dark UI has to reach past the
  product to the operating system, which lands them in forced colours below.
- **Forced colours: usable, with two graphics-only signals lost.** At `forced-colors: active`
  every control keeps a border, every figure stays legible, and the layout holds
  (`media/forced-colors-board.png`). What disappears is the allocation rows' colour swatches
  (`span.choice-row__swatch`, `forced-color-adjust: auto`, painted background only, 6×46px) and
  the fills on the week meter and the season strip. The swatches are `aria-hidden` and every row
  is labelled in text, so nothing is lost to a screen reader; a sighted student using Windows
  High Contrast loses the visual tie between a row, its slice of the money tower and the ledger,
  and loses the meter's proportion — the "6 of 14 hours" reading — which has no text equivalent
  beside it.

### What I checked on the teacher's side that holds

Worth saying, because the educator surfaces were the ones I expected to be worst and mostly are
not (all from `keyboard/educator.log`):

- **Every educator route is fully keyboard-operable and no ring traps.** Class creation, roster,
  card printing, class overview, reading queue, per-student evidence, debrief, share-out — all
  reachable, all rings close, no repeats other than the same-name problem above.
- **The client-side route-change focus works.** The lead asked me to verify rather than trust it:
  after *Class list → Objectives*, `focus = h1 "What do you want to assess?"`; after
  *Objectives → Guide*, `focus = h1 "Plan Under Pressure"`. Both correct.
- **Grading is fully keyboard-driven and announced.** Four criteria marked with `Enter`, then
  *Save and read the next* moves focus to `h2 "Seeded Student 2"` and the live region says
  `2 of 5 · Seeded Student 2 · still to read`. That is the loop a teacher spends the most time in
  and it is the best-behaved thing in the product.
- **axe over 19 educator screens: zero violations.**

### MINOR

- **The student page's tabs claim a pattern they do not implement.** `tablist "This student's
  work"` with four `role="tab"` children; pressing `ArrowRight` four times leaves focus on
  *Evidence trail* every time, and all four tabs are separate `Tab` stops rather than one roving
  stop. Content is still reachable by `Tab`, so nobody is blocked — but a screen-reader user is
  told "tab, 1 of 4" and then the arrow keys do nothing.
- **`/educator/objectives` goes `h1` → `h3`** for all 23 objectives, with no `h2` between.
- **`/educator/try`, the teacher's "play it as a student" preview, has no `<main>` landmark** and
  lands focus on `<body>` — it is the one educator route not wrapped in `EducatorShell`.
- **A refused code is announced but the field is not marked invalid.** Both errors at the class
  door reach a `role="alert"` and are spoken assertively — `[assertive] No class with that code.`
  and `[assertive] That did not match. Check it and try again.` — which is the important half and
  it works (`probes/form-errors.log`). What is missing is the other half: the field reads
  `textbox "CLASS CODE" [invalid="false"]` and carries no `aria-describedby` to the alert, so a
  student who tabs back to the box hears no error on it and has to remember what was said.
- **The class-code field narrates every keystroke.** `[polite] That is 1 — a class code is 5.`,
  `That is 2 —`, `That is 3 —`, `That is 4 —`. Four interruptions before the student has finished
  typing five characters. (`screenreader/basketball-announcements.txt`)
- **Two live regions say the same thing on the plan board.** `Still to give a job $3,100.` and
  `$3,100 still has no job.` fire together on every change, so every allocation is announced
  twice in slightly different words.
- **The Pop-Up's tray counter announces a bare number.** `[polite] 3`, `[polite] 1` — an
  `<output>` with no unit and no cost, on the screen where trays are the decision.
- **No skip link.** Every run screen costs three tab stops (app mark, *The four payments*, the
  seat control) before the stage; every educator screen costs five. Not a blocker at this size,
  but it is the reason a keyboard user re-walks the chrome after every focus loss above.
- **The allocation row's note is not part of the spinbutton's accessible name or description.**
  In forms mode a student hears `spinbutton "Sports-media course" valuetext="$0"` and not the
  line under it saying what that amount currently buys.

## Does using the reading tools cost a student anything, and does the product say so?

The brief asks this directly, and the answer has two halves that point opposite ways.

**Read-aloud and the glossary cost nothing, and the product's claim about that is true.** Verified
three ways: the directory imports nothing that could carry a record off the device
(`nothingRecorded.test.ts` enforces it, and I re-checked the imports myself); a browser run using
both tools produced an attempt log with no reading event in it; and grepping this device's whole
`localStorage` for `READ_ALOUD`, `readAloud`, `glossary`, `speech` returns zero. The educator
guide's row — *"You turn nothing on… it stays on the device… who used it is never recorded"* — is
accurate clause by clause. That is the right design and it is honestly described.

**The hint ladder is a different thing wearing the same coat, and there the product is not
honest.** *Show me one step* records `direct_scaffold`; *Show the answer and keep going* records
`answer_supplied`, which scores zero on that micro-skill. Both are correct as assessment. What is
not correct is what the child is told: in Basketball they are told *"You worked this out."*
(MAJOR 4). A student who cannot tell which of the two kinds of help they just took cannot make an
informed choice about taking it — and the one who most needs the ladder is the one most likely to
be reading that sentence.

**One thing the tools do cost is screen.** MAJOR 8 and MAJOR 11: the control that offers the
accommodation is painted over the run, including over another control at phone width. An
accommodation that hides the task is a smaller accommodation than it looks.

## The one rule underneath most of this

Eleven of the fifty recorded screen changes left focus on `<body>`, and every one of them is the
same shape.

The product has a good hook for the change it thought about. `useStageArrival` moves focus to the
new heading whenever `stage` changes; `StageShell` does the same on `focusKey`; `EducatorShell`
does it on `pathname`. All three were verified working. What none of them covers is a change
**inside** a screen — a component replacing itself in place while the URL, the stage and the
`focusKey` all stay exactly as they were:

| The in-place change | What the student loses |
|---|---|
| the arithmetic `Check` collapsing into its answer | the verdict, and their place — ×6 |
| the hint opening under the button that asked for it | the hint — ×2 |
| *Leave this run* becoming two buttons | the warning, and the safe default |
| the tips-jar beat closing and the standing order appearing | the fact that a new decision exists |
| *Create the class* becoming *Here is the code.* | the code and the key, announced to nobody |

One rule closes all of them, and it is the rule the codebase already believes in everywhere
else: **when a control removes itself, something has to take focus, and the thing it says has to
outlive the press.** In practice that is two lines per site — move focus to the element that
replaced it, and render the verdict into a live region that is not inside the subtree being
unmounted.

That is why this report has three BLOCKERs and no axe findings. Every one of these is invisible
to a rule-based scanner: the DOM is valid before the press and valid after it, and the defect
lives in the half-second between.

## What I could not measure

Said plainly rather than left as a silence:

- **I never heard the product speak.** This container has `window.speechSynthesis` and
  `getVoices().length === 0`, so read-aloud cannot produce sound here. I proved the control is
  keyboard-reachable, that it degrades honestly (*"This computer has no voice, so it cannot read
  out loud. Words still works."*), that the preference persists across screens on one device key,
  and — by stubbing a voice into the page — that the reader is wired to the `<main>` landmark and
  restarts on a screen change. **Whether the voice is intelligible on a school Chromebook is not
  established by anything in this report.** It needs a Chromebook and a person.
- **No real screen reader was run.** Everything under "what a student hears" is the accessibility
  tree Chromium exposes plus every mutation inside a live region, timestamped. That is a good
  proxy and it is not NVDA or VoiceOver. Where the two differ, believe the screen reader.
- **The 400% device-scale emulation was abandoned.** `deviceScaleFactor: 4` full-page capture was
  starving a box already at load 225, so 400% is reported at its CSS-pixel equivalent (320px
  viewport), which is how WCAG 1.4.10 defines the condition. The device-scale run would have
  added rendering realism, not layout facts.
- **No student was in the room.** Every claim here is about a machine's behaviour. Whether a
  twelve-year-old using a screen reader can finish this in a period is a question a pilot
  answers, and the same pilot gate the rest of this product is waiting on.

## Verdict

The product is in genuinely good shape on everything a rule can check and on everything a
between-screens design decision can carry: 67 screens with zero axe violations, focus managed on
arrival at nineteen of twenty-one stage changes, a live announcement stream a blind student can
finish the written defence on, an accommodation that honestly costs them nothing, a complete
reduced-motion kill switch, and no time limit anywhere. Two complete runs and a teacher's whole
setup-to-grading pass were played with no mouse at all, and every control was reachable.

What it has not done is the inside-a-screen half of the same job. A student who cannot see the
screen can start this and can finish it, but six times in a run they will press the one button on
the screen and be told nothing at all, and twice — the two rungs of the hint ladder — that
happens on the controls built specifically for the students who are struggling. On a shared
Chromebook, one reflexive Tab-then-Enter after *Leave this run* destroys the work. At the width a
teacher with low vision actually uses, the rubric they mark against renders in a zero-pixel
column.

None of that is unfixable, and most of it is the same two lines repeated at eleven sites. But
"can every student in a class be assessed with this" is not yet yes, and the students it is not
yes for are exactly the ones the accommodations were built for.

**GO WITH CONDITIONS** — ship to a sighted, mouse-or-keyboard classroom; do not put it in front
of a screen-reader user until the in-place changes speak. **The single largest barrier: pressing
*Check* with the right answer, or pressing either rung of the hint ladder, throws focus to
`<body>` and announces nothing — so on eight of the run's decision points a blind student cannot
find out what the product just did, and the hint they asked for is never read to them.**
