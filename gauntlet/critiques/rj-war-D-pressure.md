# Going Live

**A flash-sale livestream that won't pause itself, so the student learns to — pinning every push to a tray in plain sight, reading two sources that disagree about what's actually true, and deciding with the pressure still on the screen, not after it's gone.**

**Carries** `judge-a-claim` (BOW-B5, `full` → 1.4) · `notice-influence` (BOW-B6, `full` → 1.5)
**Budget** ≈2,050 words of student-facing text / ≈24 minutes

---

## Verbs

**PAUSE · OPEN · PIN · TAG · CITE · DECIDE**

---

## The premise

*As the student reads it:*

> It's Thursday, 7:58 p.m. Dez is about to go live on SwapMeet Live — the channel where Dez sells whatever's in the back room, one drop at a time, first-come, first-served, tonight it's wireless earbuds. You've got $50 of your own money and the stream is already loading. Chat is already moving. A counter is already ticking down how many are left. None of it waits for you to catch up, and none of it has to — you can freeze the whole room the second you walk in and it will sit there, silent, for as long as you want. What it won't do is agree with itself. Dez says one thing about these earbuds. The box they actually come in says something else. A kid who already bought a pair says a third thing. Somebody in this room is wrong, on purpose, and you're the only one checking.

---

## Signature mechanic

**The room.** The screen splits top-to-bottom, always both halves visible, never a modal covering one to show the other. The top 55% is **the feed**: a host window (a static-but-blinking illustrated avatar of Dez, captions appearing under it every 15–25 seconds — "Basically the ones the pros use, for a third of the price," "Ten hours, y'all. TEN," "I don't restock these — when they're gone, they're gone"), a **chat column** appending one line every 3–5 seconds ("yoooo pulling the trigger," "wait fr only 13 left??," "i got mine last month so good," from handles the student never meets again), a **stock widget** ("37 CLAIMED — 13 LEFT," decrementing on a fixed schedule as the stream runs), and a **price widget** ("$34 — was $59.99 — price resets in 04:58," the clock visibly running down). Nothing here is invented for difficulty: every element is a thing a real livestream shopping drop actually shows.

**The desk.** The bottom 45% is fixed, static, and **never blocked by the feed, never gated by time** — five cards, open from the first second: (1) Comet Audio's own spec sheet — "Playback: 5 hours per charge, 20 with the case. Water resistance: IPX4, splash resistant. Not rated for submersion or shower use." (2) *Gadget Ledger*, an independent outlet, dated three weeks ago — "We ran three pairs through our battery rig: 4h51m, 5h04m, 5h12m. IPX4 held up to light rain; we would not wear these in the shower. Gadget Ledger takes no payment from brands it reviews." (3) Dez's own listing — "TEN HOURS OF PURE SOUND 🔥 Bluetooth 5.3, 45mAh cell, ENC dual mic, shower-safe up to IPX4! These sell out every drop, get in now." (4) A friend's real DM thread — Robbie: "battery is NOT 10 hrs tho lol more like 5. also do NOT shower with them, I got water in the case door and it glitched." (5) Dez's Returns & Shipping page — "Returns accepted within 90 days, no questions asked, prepaid label provided." Cards (1), (2) and (4) agree with each other and disagree with (3). Card (5) is boring, unglamorous, and true.

**The tray.** A docked bar at the very bottom reads **WHAT'S PUSHING ME**. Any feed element — a chat line, the stock number, the price clock, a Dez caption — can be grabbed (drag on pointer; a focusable "Pin this" action on keyboard) and dropped into the tray. Dropping opens a four-option tag menu — **URGENCY · CROWD · DEAL-FRAMING · HOST SAYS** — one required tag per pin. Pinned items sit in the tray as small labeled chips, visible and re-taggable, until the moment the student decides.

**The decide screen.** A fixed card, openable any time (never forced open by a timer): **BUY COMET BUDS — $34** with a Buy / Don't Buy choice, a required field — *"What number are you going on for battery life?"* — offering "10 hours (Dez's listing)," "about 5 hours (Comet Audio + Gadget Ledger)," or "I'm not sure," and a required textbox: *"What made that number more believable than the other one — and who benefits if you believe the wrong one?"* The tray is visible in the same screen, not hidden behind it.

**Decision B, twelve minutes in.** A second, smaller drop starts inside the same stream: **ADD THE PRO CASE — $9**. Dez: "everybody's grabbing this too — 214 sold in the last hour, offer's off the second I go offline." A sixth desk card appears — **Marketplace Order Log**, a page Dez does not run, updating on its own, showing the same 214 in the same window. This claim is **true**. The decide screen repeats the shape: Add / Skip, a tag-driven prompt (*"Name one thing that was trying to move you faster than you wanted to go — and what you did about it"*), tray still visible.

**The find-out.** After each decide screen, an *ONE WEEK LATER* card appears for every student, buyers and non-buyers alike: a photo of the box's real printed spec panel (5 hrs, IPX4 — matching Comet Audio and *Gadget Ledger*, not Dez), and, for Decision B, the stream's actual final tally, 341 Pro Cases sold before Dez signed off — confirming the crowd number was real. Nobody is denied the resolution because of which way they decided.

**Why the clock is never the grade.** PAUSE is the first focusable control on the screen, always present, bound to a dedicated always-visible button and a documented key. Pausing freezes the chat, the stock number, and the price clock — as text, with a stream-time stamp, not a wall-clock — and the desk was never animated in the first place. A student can pause on entry and never resume: every PIN, OPEN, CITE and DECIDE act is available at full pause, indefinitely. Nothing in any evidence rule below reads real elapsed time, order-of-arrival speed, or whether the stock hit zero before the student acted. The stock hitting zero is a fact to reason with (Dez really did sell out), never a lockout. What a sighted, unhurried mouse user *feels* — the chat scrolling, the clock visibly running — a paused or screen-reader session simply does not produce, and that asymmetry is fine precisely because no row scores the feeling.

**Why this isn't the desk in a hoodie.** The desk exists, but nothing is flipped, bound to a question, or filed — a student can read all five cards in any order or none, and doing so produces nothing by itself. The scored act is catching a moving thing and naming what it is *while it is still there to be caught*, and separately, reading two sources that contradict each other and saying which one to act on. Neither is sorting money into bins, and neither is placing an object on a fixed axis of days.

*Rough word accounting:* premise 150 · captions/chat ≈330 · widgets ≈60 · five desk cards ≈550 · decide-A prompts ≈120 · epilogue-A ≈90 · Decision B feed + card ≈210 · decide-B prompts ≈100 · epilogue-B ≈80 · tray/tag chrome ≈80 → **≈1,770**, leaving margin under 2,200.

## The trap

**Where a suspicious student gets it wrong.** A student who (correctly) catches Dez lying about battery life in Decision A carries that distrust into Decision B. Chat's "214 sold in the last hour" reads exactly like the same trick — a number, a countdown, urgency — so they tag it CROWD (correct) but never open the Marketplace Order Log, and write "Dez is lying again, nobody bought 214 cases." The Order Log is real, independent of Dez, and shows 214. This student is wrong, on the record, for a nameable reason: they stopped checking claim-by-claim and started pattern-matching on "feels like Dez." Distrust was the right call once and got generalized into a rule, which is exactly the failure this world is built to let happen.

**Where a credulous student gets it wrong.** A student who finds Dez's listing — the most detailed, most jargon-heavy of the five cards ("Bluetooth 5.3, 45mAh cell, ENC dual mic") — never opens the spec sheet or *Gadget Ledger*, cites "10 hours (Dez's listing)," and writes "the earbuds sound great and lots of people are buying them so they're probably good." Nothing in that sentence names a number, a source, or a beneficiary. This student is wrong for a different nameable reason: more detail read as more true, and a crowd read as a fact-check.

Both are real, both are routed below, and neither is the world's obviously-correct posture — buying and not-buying are never themselves scored.

## Proposed evidence requirements

### `judge-a-claim` (BOW-B5 · `read-and-judge` · explanation required)

| Row | Kind | Observable rule | Misconception if absent | Produced by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| JC-ER1 · Reads both sides | decision | Opens at least two desk cards authored as disagreeing on the same fact before the DECIDE act for that window commits | A review is evidence | `openSource` on ≥2 cards flagged `conflictsWith` each other, before `decide` |
| JC-ER2 · Names who benefits | decision | Before deciding, tags the source they cite with a stake — seller/host, independent, or peer-who-already-has-it | A review is evidence | `sourceStakeTagged` on the card matched by `citeFigure` |
| JC-ER3 · Cites the checked number, not the loud one | decision | The figure recorded at CITE matches the figure the two independent/agreeing sources give, or the explanation states why they're overriding it | More detail means more true | `citeFigure` compared to the authored ground-truth figure on the agreeing cards |
| JC-ER4 · Checks the claim that felt true too | decision | Opens the neutral verification source for **both** windows — *Gadget Ledger* in A, the Order Log in B — not only the one they already doubted | — | `openSource` on both neutral cards across both windows |
| JC-ER5 · Explains the credibility call (explanation) | explanation | Names a concrete difference between the two sources and names who benefits if the less-credible one is believed; restating either claim scores at the bottom | A review is evidence | free text at `decide`, scored by a person against JC-ER3's citation |

### `notice-influence` (BOW-B6 · `choose-under-pressure` · explanation required)

| Row | Kind | Observable rule | Misconception if absent | Produced by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NI-ER1 · Catches the push while it's live | decision | Pins ≥3 feed elements spanning ≥3 distinct tags before either decide screen closes | Ads don't work on me | `pinItem` events, tag field, ≥3 distinct values |
| NI-ER2 · Tags it as what it actually is | decision | The tag chosen for each of a fixed reference set (price clock, stock counter, "214 sold" chat line) matches that item's authored category, not merely any tag | — | `pinItem.tagChosen` compared to `feedItemAppended.groundTruthTag` |
| NI-ER3 · Decides with the push still in view | decision | At `decide`, at least one pin from that window's tray is present in the snapshot taken at commit | — | `decide.traySnapshot` non-empty |

## The reflex path

A student not engaging: opens the world, never touches PAUSE, taps the three biggest things on screen into the tray as they scroll by — the price clock, the stock number, one chat line — accepting whichever tag sits first in the menu (URGENCY) for all three without reading which fits, opens no desk card, taps **Buy** the instant it's available, leaves the citation on "I'm not sure," types "I bought it because it was on sale and everyone else was buying it too," then repeats the identical pin-everything-URGENCY pattern at Decision B and taps **Add**.

What it scores, honestly:

- **JC-ER1, JC-ER2, JC-ER4** — fail. Zero desk cards opened; no stake tagged; neither neutral source checked.
- **JC-ER3** — the citation is "not sure," logged as unsourced; scores at the bottom, not blocked.
- **JC-ER5** — the written line names a push, not a source difference or a beneficiary; scores low, at real risk if a scorer only checks "wrote something" — this rubric's language (differentiator + named beneficiary) is what keeps that honest, not the mechanic alone.
- **NI-ER1** — fails. Three pins, but one tag repeated three times, not three distinct tags. This is exactly why the rule requires distinct tags rather than a pin count.
- **NI-ER2** — fails on the "214 sold" chat line specifically: it is authored CROWD, and reflex-tagging labels it URGENCY, the wrong category, catchable even though the price clock and stock number happen to land on URGENCY correctly.
- **NI-ER3** — **passes**, honestly. The tray was never cleared, so it's still in view at commit. This row is a structural floor, not a reasoning check, and it isn't meant to fail reflex by itself.
- **NI-ER4** — fails. The B-window tags are the same values in the same slots as A's, on a structurally different item.
- **NI-ER5** — fails. "Everyone else was buying it too" names the push but states no response to it — the purchase happened *because of* the push, not *accounting for* it, which the rule requires.

Two rows pass on a reflex run (JC-ER3's unsourced-but-logged state and NI-ER3's structural floor); none of the rows that require an actual comparison, an actual source check, or an actual differentiated response pass, and no row this student clears reaches the top of the scale.

## Accessibility

The construct is: a moving thing, a name for what kind of push it is, a claim, a competing claim, and a choice about which to act on. All five are text underneath the animation, never carried only by motion or sound, so all five survive intact.

**Keyboard.** PAUSE is the first tab-stop on the page, with a dedicated documented key in addition to the visible button. Every feed item, once appended, is a focusable list entry carrying a "Pin this" action (Enter/Space); the tag menu is a standard radio group navigable by arrow keys. The desk cards are standard disclosure buttons (`aria-expanded`). The decide screen is ordinary form controls — radio group, a labeled combobox for the cited figure, a labeled textarea. Nothing requires a drag; every drag has a keyboard-actionable equivalent doing the identical thing to the identical data.

**Screen reader.** The animated, auto-scrolling feed is a convenience layer over a **feed log** — an ordered list, `aria-live` off by default, driven by the same event stream (`feedItemAppended`) the visual chat is drawn from. A screen-reader student navigates it at their own pace like any list, which — combined with PAUSE defaulting the whole room to still on entry — means they are reading the identical sequence of chat lines, host captions, stock readings and price readings a sighted student watched animate, in the same order, with nothing summarized or dropped. The stock and price widgets are always plain text with a stream-time stamp ("Stock: 13 left, as of message 42"), not a bare animated number, so the count a screen reader announces is the same count a sighted eye tracks. Pinning from the feed log opens the same tag radio group. The desk was already plain headed sections with real text — no adaptation needed there at all.

**What is and isn't lost.** No evidence rule above reads reaction time, arrival order relative to real elapsed time, or whether an item was caught before it scrolled off-screen — the feed log persists as a growing list, so a pin made two minutes or twenty minutes after an item appeared counts identically. What a screen-reader or keyboard student does not get is the felt urgency of watching a number visibly count down — and that is acceptable specifically because the felt urgency is never the thing any row scores. What they get instead, byte-for-byte, is the same claims, the same disagreement between sources, and the same tray of what's pushing them, which is the actual construct.

**Reduced motion / muted audio.** Chat "scrolling" is items appended to a static list, not motion; the stock and price "ticking" are timestamped text updates, not animation. There is no audio channel carrying anything load-bearing — Dez's captions are always text.

**200% zoom / 640px.** Feed and desk stack as two full-width panels reachable by a tab pair; the tray becomes a docked bar with a count badge ("3 pinned"); the decide screen is a single-column form. Every pinned or cited item carries its own text label, so nothing depends on spatial position surviving the reflow.

**No color-only meaning.** Tags are text labels on the chips, not color swatches; the false-versus-true distinction between cards is never color-coded — it has to be read.

## What it costs to build

**New UI primitives.**
- **Live Feed List** — dual-rendered: an animated auto-appending scroll for sighted/motion-ok sessions, and a static, fully-navigable list (the feed log) for reduced-motion and screen-reader sessions, both reading off one event stream.
- **Pin Tray** — a persistent dock, pointer-drag and keyboard-actionable, holding re-taggable chips until decide.
- **Tag Picker** — a four-option control attachable to any feed item.
- **Desk / Source Card** — a disclosure card carrying authored metadata (`conflictsWith` other cards, a `stake`) beyond its display text, reusable by any future `read-and-judge` world.
- **Cite control** — a combobox on the decide screen bound to figures that exist verbatim in open desk sources, plus an explicit "not sure" state, so an unsourced citation is a distinct logged state rather than a silent skip.
- **Decide screen** — combined choice + citation + required explanation, with the tray visible in the same view, reusable pattern.
- **Window state** — a lightweight two-window model (Decision A / Decision B) so pin and tag behavior can be compared window-to-window for NI-ER4; nothing in the existing three mechanical families tracks this.

**New log events.**
- `feedPause` / `feedResume` (streamTime, not wall-clock)
- `feedItemAppended` (id, kind, text, groundTruthTag, streamTime)
- `pinItem` (feedItemId, tagChosen, windowId, streamTime)
- `openSource` (sourceId, windowId, streamTime)
- `sourceStakeTagged` (sourceId, stakeChosen)
- `citeFigure` (windowId, figureValue | "unsourced", sourceIdOfFigure)
- `decide` (windowId, choice, traySnapshot)
- `explanationSubmitted` (windowId, rowId, text)
- `epilogueViewed` (windowId)

**New observer surface.** An observer that reads pairs (which two sources were opened, whether they were authored as disagreeing) and window-to-window diffs (NI-ER4), which is a different shape than the single-timeline or single-ledger reads the shipped worlds use — genuinely new evaluation logic, not a re-skin of an existing observer.
