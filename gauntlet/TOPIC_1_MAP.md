# Topic 1 — the six objectives, and what each actually needs

**Judged under `COVERAGE_COURT_CONTRACT.md`, from the spine as it ships. 19 Aug 2026.**

Both flagship Worlds live in this topic and it reads **1 of 6**. That is the portfolio's loudest number, and it is not a build backlog problem: **four of Topic 1's competencies have zero authored evidence requirements**, and the one objective with everything written has two shipped worlds that deliberately refuse to produce it.

## What the map says that the count does not

**1. Topic 1 is a content problem before it is a build problem.** Five objectives are dark and four of them cannot be routed by any world anybody could build tomorrow, because nobody has written what would count. That is the opposite of Modules 2, 3 and 5, where the rows exist and the worlds do not.

**2. 1.4 and 1.5 are one build.** `judge-a-claim` is `full` on 1.4 and `partial` on 1.5; `notice-influence` is `full` on 1.5. Both are about a student deciding what to believe and who is pushing them. A single world in the read-and-judge family is the only place in this entire portfolio where **one build closes two objectives in the topic that needs them most** — and it is the family the mechanics war never designed for, because the brief anchored designers on workbench, timeline and inspection desk and nobody read the fourth as its own thing.

**3. 1.1 is nearly free, and the freeness is a trap worth checking.** `save-toward-a-goal` has all five rows written. Both shipped worlds name it in their *not produced* lists, and the reason is not laziness: *"The size of the cut, whether it survives the generator and what it is for are the student's strategy, and this world refuses to grade a strategy."* That refusal is the same principle that keeps `neutrality.test.ts` from rewarding a bigger cushion, and it is right. A world that produces `save-toward-a-goal` has to grade *whether the goal was set as a per-period number and held under competition* — not whether it was ambitious. Then 1.1 needs a completion rule, and `mappingIntegrity.test.ts` names 1.1 as capped precisely because *"nothing in the model reaches a savings goal under competition."* Producing `save-toward-a-goal` is what makes that sentence false; the rule may only be written after it is.

**4. 1.6 needs a competency change, not just a rubric.** `choose-how-to-pay` reaches one payment method. NYSED names four and asks for advantages, disadvantages, risks *and* consumer protections on each. This is the one Topic 1 objective where the honest answer might stay `partial` — and the decision is whether BOW wants a competency about comparing payment instruments at all, which is a product question, not a coverage one.

**5. Nothing here is fixed by a reasoning box.** Every one of these five needs a student to do something a world does not currently ask. That is the correct shape of a coverage gap, and it is why the count has not moved by writing rows.

## Order of attack

1. **The read-and-judge world** — 1.4 + 1.5, the empty mechanical family, and four competencies portfolio-wide (`judge-a-claim`, `compare-earning-paths`, `protect-your-information`, `weigh-investment-risk`) that currently have no home. Highest value per build anywhere in the product.
2. **`save-toward-a-goal` in a world** — 1.1, on rows that already exist, with the strategy-grading trap named above as the design constraint.
3. **`explain-different-outcomes`** — 1.2. `compare-two-lives` has one candidate world already (Kiln, Module 5); whether a Topic 1 build shares that shape or takes a different motif is a mechanics question, not a coverage one.
4. **1.6** — decide whether `choose-how-to-pay` becomes a four-method comparison or 1.6 publishes an honest permanent `partial`.

**The rubrics for 1.4 and 1.5 are not written before the mechanics war — they are written by it.** `is-the-add-on-worth-it` is the standing lesson: rows authored away from a world, then a world designed against a version of them, then a court scoring a third version. The requirements and the acts that produce them get designed together or they drift apart before either ships.
