# Director's read — all three, from the renders

`READ-B.md` was written from B's render before A and C reported, so it is on the record as an
uncontaminated first reading. This is the comparative pass. The independent visual and evidence
courts are running against the same images and have not been told any of this; where they
disagree with me, they are the ones who looked without a stake.

## What each one actually is

**A — cinematic environmental realism.** The strongest *place* of the three by a wide margin. Real
depth: three stalls at three distances with their own awnings and their own lit interiors, string
lights on catenary wire, wet ground doubling the lamps, silhouettes with necks, hems, a child
holding an adult's hand. The states pass is the best argument for the whole doctrine — 18:05 sparse
and blue, 19:35 genuinely crowded, 20:55 thinning with the trays gone. Nothing in this product has
ever looked like that.

**B — stylized management game.** The most *charming* and the most legible per-object. Trays as
steel pans, chits on a rail, a ticket hanging at the pass, a cook working behind the window.
Everything is nameable at a glance.

**C — baked environment, live instruments.** The best *instruments* and the best thinking. The
ticket at the pass with a torn edge and a `SOLD OUT` stamp is the single best-drawn object in the
war. The plate rack is three trays of ten wells, so the stock reads as *the trays the student
bought* rather than as thirty anonymous dots. "Stamp & serve" names a physical act instead of a
system action. It is also the only direction that rendered 1024×600, 400% zoom and a
failed-image fallback rather than asserting they would work.

## The failure all three share, and it is the important one

**Every one of them is a picture with a dashboard under it.** The brief said in as many words that
"beautiful image + traditional form on top" is not the best outcome, and all three delivered
exactly that: a scene band across the top, a control band across the bottom, and a hard horizontal
seam between them. A is the most extreme — below the seam is a flat brown rectangle carrying the
same three-column layout that ships today, so A is literally the current screen with a beautiful
picture pasted above it. C bridges best (chits hang *in* the baked air, the sill line reads as an
object, the ticket overlaps the seam) but it is still two rooms.

The counter is not in the world. It is a strip below the world. Nothing the student touches is lit
by the lamps they can see.

Two smaller shared failures. The top of every one of these screens is stacked chrome — C has three
separate strips (HUD, kicker, status) before the world begins, and today's screen has a headline
four times the size of anything it introduces. And the plate row in A is dark rings on dark brown,
which is the least legible representation of the most important number on the screen.

## Where I land, subject to the courts

**C's architecture wins.** The baked/live split is the only one of the three theses that is a rule
rather than a taste — *baked if it must only be believed, live if it must be read, counted,
pressed or announced* — and a rule is what survives six more worlds and two years of maintenance.
It also closes the evidence channel structurally: a plate that never varies with world state
cannot leak stock, crowd or the answer, which is a stronger guarantee than reviewer vigilance.

**But C's bake is not good enough**, and A's is. C's market is a soft smear; A's is a place. The
right move is C's architecture with A's environment craft baked into the plate — A already ships
`assets/lane-master.html`, a real three-grade drawing, as its authoring source.

**From B, take the register check rather than the register.** B is right that every object should
be nameable at a glance, and B is also the one most at risk of reading as a phone puzzle game. Its
pans-and-chits vocabulary is already present in C in a more grown-up hand.

## What the synthesis has to fix that none of them fixed

1. **One room, not two.** The sill in the foreground, the lane behind it, and the controls sitting
   *on the counter* in the same perspective and the same light. If the serve button does not cast
   a shadow onto a surface that exists in the scene, the seam is still there.
2. **Kill the stacked chrome.** One bar, and the world starts immediately under it.
3. **The plate rack is C's**, drawn as trays, and it must be the most legible object on the screen
   rather than the least.
4. **The lane count stays bucketed and the plates stay countable** — never the reverse, and never
   both countable in the same units.

## What I am not deciding here

Whether the crowd's drawn density is a leak. A's lane visibly thickens and thins with the real
crowd, which is the most alive thing in the war and also the thing most likely to be an answer
key. I have not counted the figures against the model, and I am not going to decide it from an
impression — the evidence court is counting.
